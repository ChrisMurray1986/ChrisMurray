The situation in one paragraph

Meridian is a strong-foundation, weak-connective-tissue revenue cycle. Epic is well-deployed (linkage, connectivity, and write-back gates largely pass), both AI point solutions are performing and worth expanding, and staff competence is not the problem. What the assessment found instead is a consistent pattern at the seams: knowledge that lives in people instead of systems (filing limits in analyst memory, payer bulletins in inboxes, the underpayment tracker in an Access database), feedback loops that don't close (no defect attribution to the front end, root-cause analysis that stops at "payer," the same top-5 denial categories for eight quarters), and governance that observes rather than decides (committee theater, no AI governance stack, capital asks folded into IT). These seam failures — not any single department's performance — are what lock the automation portfolio at 0 GO / 56 CONDITIONAL / 43 DEFER today.

Five headline findings

  1. Codify the knowledge that expires accounts ($32M/yr chain). Filing and appeal limits exist only in analyst memory (confirmed TFL write-offs $1.9M/yr — the visible tip); the contract engine has never been parallel-price verified ($19.2M underpayment pool blind). One 90-day codification program addresses both.
  2. Denials are managed, not prevented. Denial reporting stops at CARC codes with no owner attribution; the prevention squad has been "paused for backlog" three consecutive quarters; rebill-first is the stated team standard. The $25.9M prevention pool is structurally untouchable until attribution exists.
  3. Three payers' denials are invisible. Zero-pay 835s auto-adjust to contractual for three payers (hidden-denial audit confirmed) — the reported denial rate is understated and $6M/yr of recovery is off the books.
  4. The point solutions outran the operating model. Fathom and Humata perform, but seven pre-existing bots run unregistered (one dead six weeks before discovery), automation rumors have already cost two senior coders, and no org-level AI governance exists — the next 70 launches would inherit this unless fixed first.
  5. Measurement can't referee. Two AR-days definitions in one executive deck; metrics re-derived per report from personal SQL. Every improvement claim — human or AI — is unprovable until the KPI dictionary and certified metric layer exist.

What we recommend (the program in one view)

A single two-year program on four tracks: F Foundation (governance, data, fabric — $2.7M, Q1–Q2), O Operational fixes from this assessment's findings ($0.8M, largely 30–90 day sprints, several cash-positive in-quarter), A AI & automation launches (70 use cases, Q3–Q8, budget-optimized sequence), and V Vendor actions (expand Fathom/Humata, renegotiate data rights, licenses). Total $18.3M over 8 quarters; value crosses cumulative investment in Q5; exit at $69.1M/yr net. Full portfolio in §Initiative portfolio; quarter-by-quarter in §Implementation plan.

Governance, measurement & caveats

  • Operating rhythm: quarterly re-assessment (both instruments) → regenerated packet → 2-hour council with pre-read; true-up first on every agenda. The quarter-over-quarter diff is the program status report.
  • Hard rules: baseline before every go-live; every launch starts in recommend mode; a remediation is done only when its facet/finding score moves; one benefit ledger, every dollar counted once, value net of run cost; role-level findings aggregate-only.
  • Risk watchlist: change capacity (10–14 go-lives/quarter is the true constraint — council may stretch Phase 3 without materially changing the curve); notes-access approval timing (1-quarter float held); criteria-license negotiation (blockable — fallback is procedural/policy argumentation); estimate bands are wide by design and correct in public.
  • What we did not find: no KILL-class blockers, no compliance emergencies beyond the 60-day register discipline (O10), and no case for a data-platform purchase — Clarity/Caboodle plus a scoped augmentation layer passes every gate this program needs.
