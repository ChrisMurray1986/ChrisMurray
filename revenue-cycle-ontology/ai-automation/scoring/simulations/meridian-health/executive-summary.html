Where Meridian starts — the point solutions were the right first moves

The readiness assessment (98 facts, scored against "demo it this week" evidence) shows a stronger starting position than a typical system: Epic's native surfaces already satisfy the data-linkage, write-back, ordering-hook, and eligibility/remit connectivity gates, and the Fathom and Humata deployments bought three things beyond their direct value: structured auth records, coding validation sets, and — most importantly — organizational muscle for AI change management and vendor governance. The plan keeps both vendors and expands them (Fathom to inpatient/professional coding in Q6, Humata to additional payers in Q6–Q7) rather than rebuilding what they cover.

What the point solutions did not buy: the shared foundations. No organization-level AI governance (logging, monitoring, demotion authority), no denial-label attribution, no approved AI access to the clinical note corpus, an expected-pay engine that is loaded but has never been verification-priced, and no citation-verification capability. Every one of these gates multiple use cases — that is why the portfolio scores 0 GO today despite the strong base: 56 use cases are one small step away (CONDITIONAL), 43 need a foundation fixed first (DEFER), and none are killed.

The five findings that set the plan

  1. Governance is the single biggest lever: standing up the AI governance stack unlocks $61.6M/yr held behind it (64 use cases). It is ~$1.2M of Q1–Q2 work.
  2. Exception-path standardization unlocks $48.2M/yr (49 use cases) — a 90-day process sprint, not a technology purchase.
  3. The label pipeline and clinical-note access unlock the analytics half of the portfolio ($37.4M and $17.3M/yr respectively) — approvals and attribution tables, not new platforms. No data-lakehouse purchase is required; Clarity/Caboodle plus a scoped augmentation layer passes every gate this plan needs.
  4. Revenue integrity is the fastest money: missing-charge detection ($5.6M/yr net), payment-variance coverage ($4.7M), coverage discovery ($3.9M), and underpayment recovery ($3.8M) all launch in Q3 with ~1-month paybacks once foundations land.
  5. Contract verification before estimates: the expected-pay engine gets parallel-price verified in Q3 (contract extraction), because estimates, variance, and underpayment work all compute against it.

What we are asking the council to approve

  1. $2.5M/quarter investment envelope for 8 quarters ($17.5M total; expected cumulative net value $62.8M within the window, $69.1M/yr run-rate at exit).
  2. The Q1 foundation slate (14 projects, $2.1M) — funded as projects with facet-score exit criteria, before any use-case build.
  3. The operating loop: named loop operator, quarterly re-assessment and investment packet, benefit-owner sign-off, baseline-before-go-live as a hard rule.
  4. Vendor posture: retain and expand Fathom and Humata; renegotiate criteria and reference licenses for programmatic use in Q1; require data-rights and telemetry clauses at every AI-vendor renewal.
  5. Autonomy policy: every launch starts in recommend mode; autonomy is earned with evidence and re-priced in the cost model (this is where review labor — the largest run cost — falls).
