# Build-Out #3 — "You Are the Culture Strategy"
## AI for personal productivity & executive leadership (Segment 3, 30–45 min)

**Slot:** Final segment of the 2-hour block
**Duration:** 40-min core design (F: 7 min framing → G: 22 min live demos → H: 8 min habit design + commitment → 3 min close into Mirror Q8)
**Theme anchors:** Executive personal AI fluency (Theme 7), workforce/team development (Theme 4), readiness gap (Theme 1)

---

## 1. Design logic — the reframe that makes this segment land

The failure mode for "AI for personal productivity" content with a C-suite audience is that it reads as *tips and tricks* — LinkedIn-listicle territory, delegable to their chief of staff. The research gives you the escape:

> At companies capturing the most AI value, **88% of managers visibly role-model AI** in decision-making and daily operations. At laggards: **25%**. (BCG, 2026)

So the segment's thesis is **not** "here are tools that save you time." It is:

> **"Your personal AI practice is the single highest-leverage culture intervention you own. Your team will not become what you mandate. They will become what they watch you do."**

Under that frame, every demo doubles as an organizational strategy artifact, and the segment completes the arc of the block: the keynote said what 2030 looks like, the Lab put the 2030 operating model and workforce in their own hands, and this segment answers *"what do I personally do about it, starting tomorrow morning?"*

**Authenticity is the differentiator.** Vendor demos are polished; yours should be real — your actual use cases, your actual prompts, including one that produces a mediocre first draft you improve live. The imperfection is the credibility.

## 3. F — The frame (7 min, script beats)

**Beat 1 — Pay off the open loop (Mirror Station 4 / Q6).** Re-display the room's Station-1 answer to *"how many times did you use GenAI yesterday?"*
> *"At the start of this session I asked you to remember your answer. Here's the room."*

**Beat 2 — The BCG reveal.**
> *"Now here's why that chart matters more than any vendor selection you'll make this year: at the companies capturing the most AI value, 88% of managers visibly role-model AI in their decisions and daily work. At the laggards, 25%. Not budgets. Not tools. Whether the boss visibly uses it."*

**Beat 3 — The Delegation Test.**
> *"You might be thinking: I have people for this. Two problems. First — 77% of CEOs call AI transformative, but only 44% believe their CIO is equipped to navigate it. You can't delegate what you can't evaluate; you'll be arbitrating AI investment cases, vendor claims, and operating-model and workforce decisions personally — you just spent 40 minutes proving that in the Lab. Second — the research says executives are structurally the SLOWEST people in their organizations to build AI fluency. Not because of age or aptitude: because your calendar is meetings and approvals, so you never get natural reps. Your analysts get fifty at-bats a day. You get zero — unless you engineer them."*

**Beat 4 — The promise + disclosure guardrail (say this before any demo).**
> *"So for the next twenty minutes I'm going to show you my actual practice — not a vendor demo, my real workflows, including where the output is mediocre. Two ground rules I follow and recommend: I use an enterprise-grade tool with data protections for anything organizational, and nothing with PHI or identifiable personnel detail ever goes in. Same rule you'll set for your teams — model it here."*

## 4. G — The live stack (22 min, 4 demos × ~5 min)

Selection criteria used: (1) operational-SVP-native — board prep, operating-model & workforce design, talent, strategy; (2) demonstrable live in ≤5 min; (3) each maps to a leadership development claim, not a productivity claim; (4) collectively they ladder in altitude — board/up (A), org/team (B), individual/down (C), self/in (D) — and from "safe" to "vulnerable," which builds the room's trust.

> **Presenter prep:** run every demo 3+ times beforehand with the same prompts; save transcripts as backup slides in case of Wi-Fi/tool failure (show the saved run and narrate). Keep prompts SHORT on screen — the lesson "good prompts are just clear delegation" is itself leadership content.

### Demo A — Board-Question Red Team (the safe opener)
**Setup line:** *"Thursday I present an AI investment case to a board. Watch what I do the night before."*
**Prompt pattern:**
> *"You are a skeptical health system board member with a finance background who has read the MIT finding that >95% of enterprise GenAI investment shows no measurable ROI. Here is my one-page investment summary [paste]. Ask me the six hardest questions you would ask, ranked by how likely they are to derail approval. Then critique my weakest answer."*
**Live moment:** answer one generated question out loud, poorly on purpose, then ask the tool to critique the answer.
**Leadership point:** *"This is rehearsal infrastructure. Every high-stakes conversation you have can now be rehearsed against a tireless, well-read opponent."*

### Demo B — The Operating-Model & Workforce Partner (the Lab callback)
**Setup line:** *"You just spent 20 minutes redesigning a pod and deciding where its people go. Here's what I do with that placemat on Monday — because the redesign is the easy part. Carrying it is the job."*
**Prompt pattern (Move 1 — pressure-test the redesign):**
> *"I'm a revenue cycle SVP. Here's my redesigned denials-and-AR pod: 94 FTE today → ~25, with agents running routine denials at execute-and-audit autonomy, a human floor of ~17 for clinical-necessity attestation and high-dollar write-offs, and five net-new roles (pod product owner, AI operations supervisor, contract intelligence, continuous-improvement lead, clinical evidence curator). Play three skeptics in turn — my compliance officer, my CFO, and a frontline denial supervisor — and give me the single hardest question each one asks. Then flag the one assumption in this design most likely to be wrong."*

**Live moment:** answer the frontline supervisor's question out loud, then push the tool into the harder half —
> *"Now build the workforce transition: a 4-quarter redeployment sequence — which roles convert first, which reqs I stop backfilling this quarter, the two reskilling paths I stand up (coder→AI-audit is short, AR-rep→contract-intelligence is long), and the three sentences I say to the team before the rumor gets there first."*

**Leadership point:** *"This isn't a productivity trick — it's how a leader does org design and change management with a partner that never gets tired of your what-ifs. The redesign your table did is a napkin; this turns it into a plan you can defend to compliance and deliver to your people. And notice the last thing I asked it for: the hardest sentence you'll say all year, rehearsed before you have to say it live."*

### Demo C — The Feedback Synthesizer (the talent-development demo)
**Setup line:** *"Leadership development, not productivity. I have a director who's technically brilliant and struggling with executive presence."* (Fictionalized composite — say so.)
**Prompt pattern:**
> *"Here are anonymized themes from a leader's 360 feedback [paste 5–6 bullet themes, no names]. Help me three ways: (1) what's the ONE developmental headline if I could only say one thing; (2) draft how I open a 30-minute conversation so this lands as investment, not criticism; (3) role-play them getting defensive and coach me through my response."*
**Live moment:** run the defensive role-play for two turns.
**Leadership point:** *"Most leaders under-invest in feedback conversations because they're hard to start. Rehearsal removes the excuse — and this is the exact pattern to teach your directors for THEIR people. Fluency compounds down the org chart."*

### Demo D — The Thinking Partner (the vulnerable closer)
**Setup line:** *"Last one is the one I actually use most, and the least glamorous: Sunday evening, 15 minutes, my week."*
**Prompt pattern:**
> *"Here is my calendar for next week [paste sanitized summary] and my three stated priorities for the quarter [paste]. Interrogate me: (1) where does my time contradict my priorities? (2) which meetings should I not attend and what should I delegate to grow WHOM? (3) what am I avoiding? Be direct."*
**Live moment:** show a real (sanitized) prior run where it caught something — e.g., a priority with zero calendar time against it. Let the mediocre parts of the output show; improve one live.
**Leadership point:** *"Notice what this actually is: a mirror with a memory and no fear of me. Nobody on your team will tell you what you're avoiding. This will."*

### Demo E (extension only) — The Room Drives
Take one volunteered use case from the audience ("give me a real situation from your week — no names") and build the prompt live with the room shouting refinements. Highest engagement risk, highest engagement reward; only run with 45 minutes.

## 5. H — Habit design + commitment (8 min)

**Beat 1 — Why good intentions fail here (2 min).**
> *"Every one of you will leave intending to do this. The research says most of you won't — not from skepticism, from calendar structure. So we're not leaving with intentions; we're leaving with one habit and one public act."*

**Beat 2 — Three habit patterns that survive an executive calendar (3 min).**
1. **The Default Switch** — the tactic CEOs are already using on their teams: GenAI *before* search, GenAI *before* asking staff for a first draft. Rewire one existing reflex rather than adding a new block to the calendar.
2. **The Standing Rehearsal** — attach AI to one recurring high-stakes event you already have (board prep, payer meetings, monthly operating review): 15 minutes of red-teaming the night before. Recurring meeting = recurring rep.
3. **The Visible Rep** (the culture play) — once a week, use AI *in front of* your team: on the screen in a leadership meeting, in a working session. Say what you're doing while you do it. *"This is the 88%-versus-25% behavior. It costs you four minutes and a little ego."*

**Beat 3 — Write it down (3 min).** Commitment card at each seat (see §7):
- My one habit (which pattern, attached to what): ______
- My one visible rep this month (what, in front of whom): ______
- The first thing I will rehearse: ______
- One person I'll tell tomorrow: ______

> *"Fold it, pocket it. In 30 seconds I'm going to ask the anonymous version on the screen."* → flows into Mirror Q8 word cloud, which closes the entire block.

## 6. The leave-behind: "Building Your Team's Fluency" one-pager

Front side — **the executive's personal starter kit:** the four demo prompt patterns from §4, verbatim, plus the two ground rules (enterprise-grade tool; no PHI/identifiable personnel data) and the three habit patterns.

Back side — **the team playbook (Theme 4 payoff):**
- **The future-built targets:** upskill >50% of employees on AI (laggards do ~20%); structured learning program with *protected time* (future-built are ~4x more likely to have one); manager role-modeling as an explicit expectation (the 88/25 stat).
- **The rev-cycle role evolution map:** coder → AI auditor / clinical-context reviewer; biller → exception strategist; denial specialist → payer-behavior analyst; front-end rep → patient financial advocate. Framing note: the retention story matters — staff increasingly stay for growth and meaningful work, so the reskilling narrative *is* the retention strategy.
- **A 90-day team starter:** (1) name the roles that convert and tell people early; (2) stand up weekly protected practice hour with real work, not tutorials; (3) every leadership meeting opens with one "AI rep of the week" shared by a different leader.

## 8. Risks & contingencies

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Live demo fails / Wi-Fi dies | Saved-transcript backup slides; narrating a real prior run is nearly as strong |
| Output is embarrassingly bad live | Feature, not bug — critique it live and fix it: that IS executive fluency. Only unrecoverable if all demos flop, hence 3+ rehearsals |
| Reads as tool endorsement / vendor pitch | Name the pattern, not the product: "any frontier-model enterprise tool does this — the practice is the point, the tool is fungible" |
| Confidentiality optics (Demo C/D content) | Fictionalized composite for C (say so explicitly); sanitized calendar for D; ground-rules beat in F covers you |
| Audience member: "our org blocks these tools" | Honest answer: that's a governance decision to revisit at exec level — "if your staff can't use AI at work, they're using consumer AI at home with your data; sanctioned beats shadow" |
| Segment drifts into tool Q&A ("which one should I buy?") | Park it: "tools change quarterly; the four patterns and three habits will outlive every one of them" |
