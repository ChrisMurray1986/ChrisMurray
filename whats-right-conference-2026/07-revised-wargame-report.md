# Wargame 2.0 — The Payer–Provider AI Contest, 2026–2030
## A doctrine-ablated strategic wargame study (14 games, pre-registered, adversarially reviewed)

**Prepared for:** AI & Automation Revenue Cycle capability lead, Huron — supporting the "What's Right In Healthcare" executive session
**Supersedes:** `06-ai-wargame-report.md` (Study 1), whose nine disclosed limitations this design was built to resolve. Study 1 is retained as the D1×W1 comparison cell.

**Design:** 14 games across a fractional factorial — three adjudication doctrines (D1 baseline: symmetric escalation cancels; D2 escalation-viable: capability wins and payer payment integrity on self-funded volume is fee revenue; D3 asymmetric-friction: payers deploy in quarters, providers in 12–24 months with stall risk) × three world-models (W1 activist regulator; W2 slow/passive, base-rate calibrated; W3 hostile/chaotic) × two independent evidence briefs (provider-side sources only; payer-side/legal sources only). Five player cells per game: Blue (4 provider archetypes), Red (3 payer archetypes with explicit P&L: ASO fee revenue, MLR, Stars, employer retention), **Green (self-funded employer coalition)**, **Vendor (PE-backed duopoly monetizing both sides)**, and a White adjudicator running a legal-signability gate, a market-diffusion ledger scored against historical base rates, and milestone lag tables — ordinal outputs only. Two out-of-frame seeds (federal preemption shock; vertical-integration wave). **15 pre-registered hypotheses** scored per game by taxonomy-blind rapporteurs; per-game reality audits; 4-lens futures panel (inside, outside, payer-strategist, legal); 4-lens adversarial peer review (payer-realism, healthcare-inertia, methodology, antitrust counsel).

**How to read this:** all probabilities are ordinal bands; where review lenses disagreed, ranges are reported. No finding refuted by 2+ reviewers survived; weakened findings appear in corrected form. Remaining limitations are in §8.

# 1. BOTTOM LINE

**The payer–provider AI contest does not end in détente, exhaustion, or regulation. It ends in a priced, contract-governed armistice with a rotating extraction frontier — and the only brake that ever fired on payer payment-integrity behavior was the employer checkbook. Never a statute, never a court, never CMS.**

For a CFO, the operational translation:

1. The instruments that will actually protect revenue through 2030 are **renewal-cycle contract paper and purchaser alliances**, not regulatory timelines.
2. **The war you are tooled for is not the war you will be fighting by 2029.** Constrained recovery value rotates within roughly one turn onto recovery-ledger surfaces — COB, subrogation, eligibility, pricing edits — that sit outside every appeal right, statute, and patient-AI tool built this cycle.
3. Study 1's two most heavily weighted futures **collapsed under design variation**: regulated equilibrium (F3, 27% in Study 1) occurred **zero times in 14 games** — including both activist-regulator games — and pure negotiated détente appeared only in the baseline-doctrine cell, enforced by employer procurement discipline, not goodwill.

## New findings (did not exist in Study 1's design)

- **Purchaser primacy** (H11: 12 supported/2 partial): the employer/ASO fiduciary channel was the first binding constraint on payer payment-integrity behavior in **all 14 games, including both activist-regulator games**. All four peer reviewers weakened it on the same ground — no GREEN-inactive control ran, so channel *primacy* is proven, channel *necessity* is not. It is a channel bet, not a world bet — and it is the largest strategic omission from every Study-1 playbook.
- **Process-term floor / corridor ceiling** (H15: the registry's only unanimous verdict, 14/14): at least one payer-signable process instrument binds at renewal in every cell — and **zero numeric denial-rate corridors were signed or even seriously proposed in signable form in 14 games**. Diffusion caveat: everything stayed signatory-level; nothing became industry infrastructure by 2030.
- **Vendor self-liquidation** (14/14): the standalone both-sides vendor model separated in every game — demerger, split, divestiture, or discounted exit — always via **private mechanisms** (procurement conflict screens, diligence, discovery), never a regulator. Hard scope limit from peer review: payer-integrated arms (Optum/Carelon-class), which hold most real both-sides capacity, were never modeled and have proven durable in reality.
- **The recovery-ledger endgame**: every rotation sequence in the study terminated on non-clinical financial-recovery surfaces. All four D3 games ended with a fully loaded successor conflict on that ledger scheduled for 2031 — unscored, outside the horizon.

# 4. BOARD BRIEF (what a CFO tells the board)

1. **Plan for contracts, not regulators.** In 14/14 games across every regulatory posture, the first constraint that bound payer behavior was purchaser/contract paper; statutes stayed decorative through 2030. Keep every needed protection in bilateral renewal terms; treat regulatory timelines as background.
2. **Fund the two moves that were non-negative in every doctrine and world:** prevention-at-source (clean-claim engineering and documentation origination — the one move the payer cannot retaliate against) and industrialized appeals aimed at the never-appealed ~40% of denials, run as a self-liquidating program.
3. **Stand up an employer-alliance desk now.** Feed self-funded employers claim-line evidence for fiduciary audits, co-draft ASO RFP riders, time asks to award cycles. The employer checkbook was the only brake that fired in any scenario; the provider who arms it rides enforcement it could never buy alone.
4. **Load every renewal from the demonstrated signable whitelist** (edit-library disclosure, change-notice floors, deemed-approval SLAs, gold-card criteria, audit rights, fee true-ups/clawbacks) plus anti-rotation scope clauses naming adjacent surfaces. Spend zero capital on numeric denial-rate corridors.
5. **Pre-position on the recovery ledger in 2026–27** — audit and reconciliation capability for COB, subrogation, eligibility, credit-balance, contract-pricing edits — before the payer's engine arrives there.
6. **Detection is won by paper, not analytics.** Negotiate quarterly adverse-action vector-mix disclosure in reporting-and-trigger form; telemetry builds ran one rotation behind the money in every game they were tried.
7. **Adopt an ownership-keyed vendor conflict standard** (ownership mapping, executed firewall addenda, termination-for-conflict rights). Assume standalone both-sides vendor dependencies carry a scheduled disruption; screen payer-integrated vendors but do not expect them to self-liquidate.
8. **Institute litigation-grade artifact hygiene from day one.** Weaponization ran through discoverable internal documents, not voluntary publication — treat every rotation memo and targeting note as a future exhibit; gate publications through independent-manager or safe-harbor structures.

# 5. STRATEGY CHAPTER

## 5.1 Robust across all tested doctrines AND worlds
1. **Prevention-at-source** — clean-claim engineering + documentation origination; compiles the payer's own disclosure obligations into a targeting package; the only unretaliable move (6 cells cited).
2. **Industrialize the never-appealed ~40% tail** (55–67% overturn base rates, no counterparty consent needed, ledger-positive from month one everywhere it ran) — while refusing symmetric contests on the payer's strongest surfaces.
3. **The abrasion ledger, cashed only at renewal** — documented abrasion converted to paid terms at renewal and *only* at renewal, in all 14 games; the enforcement clock is the contract calendar, which no world-model touches.
4. **Arm the employer/purchaser channel** — evidence, fiduciary-audit support, direct-contract options timed to RFP cycles.
5. **Pool defense infrastructure as coalition utilities — converted to independent-manager form early.** Pooling erased the casualty tier; unconverted compacts drew FTC/DOJ process that froze them for exactly the turns that mattered.
6. **For concentrated-payer books: remove disputes from existence** — prospective-payment/capitated direct contracts; a dollar never adjudicated cannot be denied, edited, or recouped; immune to any capability differential.

## 5.2 Fragile (failed everywhere tried)
- **Legal/regulatory-primary strategy** — self-liquidated within one turn in every game, including under 15+ statutes and strict enforcement; litigation pays only as a pricing mechanism converted into process-term settlements at leverage peaks.
- **Analytics-first rotation detection** — structurally one rotation behind; killed by sub-90-day edit rotation vs. 12–24-month builds; detection was achieved everywhere by contract.
- **Symmetric escalation on high-capability-gap surfaces** — every Blue actor who contested a 3+ gap surface lost the exchange; appeal theses sized to today's denial mix target yesterday's war.
- **Numeric corridors / outcome guarantees as asks** — 0/14; signals unsophistication and burns capital better spent on the whitelist.
- **Both-sides vendor dependence + ungated publication** — the fragile forms of two otherwise sound ideas (conflict-screened sourcing; gated publication).

## 5.3 Study-1 Tier-1 moves, revisited against the new evidence
| Move | Verdict | Amendment |
|---|---|---|
| Total-adverse-action measurement layer | **UPGRADED** | Extend the perimeter past clinical denials to the full recovery ledger; implement **contractually** (quarterly vector-mix disclosure with notice floors and audit triggers), not as internal analytics |
| Pre-drafted renewal packages | **UPGRADED** | Draft exclusively from the signable whitelist; add anti-rotation scope clauses naming adjacent vectors; expect to pay in term length — every concession is sold for a lock |

Comply at attestation pace; redirect the investment into **owned-data capture** (835/remittance mining, denial-vector coding, overturn cohorting) that no regulator's timeline can strand |
| Counsel screening of delegation offers | **UPGRADED** | Expand to a standing ownership-keyed conflict standard across all vendor relationships; a vendor's refusal to execute your firewall paper as drafted is itself the most valuable documented fact you can hold |

## 5.4 New moves (traceable only to Wargame 2.0 discoveries)
1. **Employer-alliance desk** (permanent function — the largest strategic omission from Study 1 playbooks)
2. **Contractual rotation detection** (reporting-and-trigger form — the form that survives the signability gate)
3. **Recovery-ledger pre-positioning** (2026–27, before the 2031 successor war)
4. **Ownership-keyed vendor conflict standard** (your screens are the enforcement mechanism)
5. **Gated-publication discipline** (who controls the artifact's frame determines shield vs. ammunition)
6. **Draft preemption-proof** (contract-anchored protections survived the preemption shock; statute-anchored ones evaporated — and since statutes never bit anyway, contract-first costs nothing even if preemption never comes)

# 6. PEER REVIEW OUTCOME (4 lenses × 8 headline findings)

No headline was refuted by 2+ lenses (nothing dropped). Best-surviving: **yield displacement** (confirmed by payer-realism and inertia lenses) and **squeezed-middle non-survival + vendor self-liquidation** (confirmed by methodology and antitrust lenses, with scope caveats). Most-weakened: **purchaser primacy** (all four lenses, same reason — no GREEN-inactive control) and the **one-attractor claim** (all four — cannot yet be distinguished from the design constants present in every cell). Every correction is incorporated in §§1–5.

**Remaining limitations (the Wargame 3.0 program):** the decisive controls were never run — no D1×W1 anchor replication, no GREEN-inactive cell, no gate-free cell, no VENDOR-inactive cell — so the armistice attractor cannot yet be distinguished from the three design constants present in every game. Also: aliased factorial with thin cells; shared-model correlation (14 games are correlated draws, not independent replications); briefing contamination on H04/H11; turn-clock/renewal-boundary alignment flattering the renewal-exclusivity finding; MA/Stars and fully-insured MLR dynamics underweighted (read findings as self-funded-book results); payer-integrated vendor forms untested; B3G3/B3G4 records patched post-hoc.

# 8. IMPLICATIONS FOR THE CONFERENCE SESSION

1. **The keynote line upgrades:** from "falling denial rates can be a trap" to **"by 2029 the denial will have changed clothes"** — the Evacuation Equilibrium (payer exit into COB/subrogation/pricing channels beyond every current tool) is the single most presentation-worthy discovery, and it's doctrine-driven, not speculative color.
2. **The live war game gains a fifth actor.** The study's strongest result is that employers are the only brake that fires. Add a Green table or a Green inject to build-out `01` — "the employer coalition just demanded clawback riders in its ASO RFP; how does your posture change?"
3. **The Monday-action list is sharper and cheaper:** employer-alliance desk, contractual vector-disclosure, recovery-ledger pre-positioning, ownership-keyed vendor screens — none requires an AI purchase, which is precisely the anti-vendor-pitch content the session promises.
4. **The meta-story is a live demonstration of AI governance:** a multi-agent analysis that pre-registered its hypotheses, ablated its own assumptions, ran adversarial review, disclosed what was weakened, and specified the experiments that could falsify its own headline. Tell the room that — it models the exact discipline the keynote asks of them.
