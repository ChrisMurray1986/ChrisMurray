# Build-Out #2 — "The Readiness Mirror"
## Live peer benchmarking threaded through the full 2-hour block

**Format:** Anonymous live polling (Slido/Mentimeter) at four moments across the 2 hours — not a standalone segment
**Total stage time consumed:** ~12–14 minutes across the block
**Theme anchors:** Readiness gap (Theme 1), pilot purgatory (Theme 2), workforce (Theme 4), executive fluency (Theme 7)

---

## 1. Design logic

- The #1 value executives seek from closed-door peer sessions is **"where do I actually stand?"** — and this room can't get honest answers from vendor benchmarks or public conference polling.
- Every question pairs a **room poll** with a **published national benchmark**, so each reveal produces two comparisons at once: *me vs. this room* and *this room vs. the nation*. That double contrast is the engagement engine.
- Threading (four short stations) beats a standalone polling block: it re-energizes the room at transitions, and each station tees up the segment that follows — the Mirror is the block's connective tissue, exactly as prioritized.
- **Anti-pitch safeguard:** every benchmark is a third-party published statistic with the source on-slide. Huron presents no proprietary numbers here.

**Standing rule (announce once at Station 1):** *"Everything is anonymous, nothing leaves the room, and I'll show you the national number only AFTER you vote — no anchoring."*

---

## 2. The four stations

### STATION 1 — Cold open, BEFORE the keynote (4 min, 3 questions)
*Purpose: establish the buy-in-vs-readiness gap as the session's premise in the room's own data — then the keynote lands on prepared ground.*

**Q1. "Where is your organization on AI in the revenue cycle?"**
- Exploring / Piloting in select areas / Deploying at scale in multiple functions / Not meaningfully started
- **Benchmark reveal:** Nationally — 53% pilots, 27% at scale (HFMA, Feb 2026). *(adversarially verified)*
- **Talk beat:** *"Nobody in this room needs convincing about AI — so I'm not going to spend one minute doing it. Look at where this room is versus the country."*

**Q2. "How prepared is your TEAM for the revenue cycle of the future?"**
- Very prepared / Somewhat prepared / Not very / Not at all
- **Benchmark reveal:** Nationally, ~7% very prepared, 44% somewhat (HFMA, Feb 2026). *(adversarially verified)*
- **Talk beat:** *"Eighty percent adoption activity. Seven percent readiness. That 73-point gap is what these two hours are about. Everything you hear from vendors targets the 80. Today targets the 7."*

**Q3. "Be honest: how many times did YOU personally use a generative AI tool YESTERDAY?"**
- 0 / 1–2 / 3–10 / It's woven through my whole day
- **No benchmark yet — hold the reveal.** *"Remember your answer. We're coming back to this number in the last segment, and I'll show you why it might be the most strategically important answer you give today."*
- (This is the setup for the BCG 88%-vs-25% payoff in Segment 3 — a deliberate open loop across the whole block.)

### STATION 2 — Keynote close / War-game entry (3 min, 2 questions)
*Purpose: convert keynote agreement into stakes, and arm the war game.*

**Q4. "What is your denial/underpayment trend over the last 24 months?"**
- Improving / Flat / Worsening / Worsening sharply
- **Benchmark reveal:** 41% of providers now face denial rates >10%, up from 30% in 2022 (Experian 2025); one system measured +50% denials/underpayments in two years as payers adopted AI review.
- **Talk beat:** *"If your line is worsening while your AI spend is rising — you're not failing. You're in an arms race. Let's go fight it."* → hands directly to the war game cold open.

**Q5. "Are payer challenges among YOUR top three stressors right now?"**
- Yes, #1 / Yes, top three / No
- **Benchmark reveal:** 88% of provider executives say yes, top three (Guidehouse/HFMA 2026).

### STATION 3 — War-game close (2 min, 1 question)
*This is the war game's closing poll (Build-Out #1 §9): "What is YOUR 2026 move?" — six move options. It belongs to both designs; run it once.*

### STATION 4 — Leadership segment open + block close (4–5 min, 3 questions)

**Q6 (opens Segment 3). Payoff of the open loop:** re-display the room's Q3 distribution (personal AI use yesterday).
- **Benchmark reveal:** At companies capturing the most AI value, **88% of managers visibly role-model AI** in decisions and daily operations — versus 25% at laggards (BCG 2026). And: senior executives are structurally the *slowest* to build fluency because their days are meetings and approvals, not automatable tasks.
- **Talk beat:** *"This chart of this room is not a productivity statistic. It's a leading indicator of which of your organizations captures AI value. Your personal practice is the culture strategy — that's this segment."*

**Q7. "Does your organization have a structured AI learning program with protected time — for your REV CYCLE team?"**
- Yes / Sort of (informal) / No
- **Benchmark reveal:** Future-built companies are ~4x more likely to run structured AI-learning programs with protected time, and plan to upskill >50% of employees vs. ~20% at laggards (BCG 2026).

**Q8 (final question of the entire block). "What will you do differently in the next 90 days?"**
- Open text, word cloud, 5 words max.
- **Talk beat (final line of the 2 hours):** *"Screenshot this wall. This is what the leaders of American revenue cycle decided in one room in one afternoon. My ask: pick the one that's yours, put a date on it, and tell one person on your team tomorrow."*

## 4. Facilitation mechanics

- **Tooling:** one Slido/Mentimeter event for the whole block, stations pre-loaded in order; QR code printed on table tents so re-entry is instant at every station. Test on venue Wi-Fi; have the presenter device on hotspot.
- **Rhythm per question:** ask → 30–45 sec voting (with countdown music or silence — not chatter) → show room result → *then* reveal national benchmark → one talk beat, max 30 seconds. Never editorialize before the vote.
- **The double-reveal discipline:** room number first, national second. The gasp lives in the gap between them.
- **Small-room fallback (<15 people or tech failure):** colored cards (A/B/C/D) at each seat + a flip chart. The word cloud (Q8) becomes a round-robin: each person says their five words aloud — arguably stronger in a small room.
- **Data capture:** export all poll results immediately after the session. They feed the follow-up note (see §5) and — aggregated and anonymized — become a genuinely scarce artifact: *a readiness snapshot of a room controlling tens of billions in NPR.*

## 5. The follow-up artifact (the Mirror's second life)

Within one week, send attendees a two-page **"What This Room Said"** summary: every room distribution side-by-side with its national benchmark, plus the Q8 commitment wall. This is the rare conference follow-up that gets forwarded internally — it is their own data, it is benchmarked, and it restates their own commitment. (Clear it with conference privacy ground rules; aggregate-only, no individual responses.)

## 6. Risks & contingencies

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Wi-Fi/tool failure | Card fallback per §4; benchmarks still present fine without live polling |
| Low participation (phones down) | Table tents with QR + facilitator votes live on screen first question to model it; first question is deliberately easy |
| Room skews far better than national benchmarks (elite audience) | Expected and useful: *"You're ahead of the country — which means your competition is each other and the payers, and the bar for 'prepared' just moved."* |
| Someone challenges a benchmark's validity | Sources are on-slide; concede freely that AKASA/Adonis/Black Book are vendor-sponsored surveys — the honesty itself differentiates the session |
| Q8 word cloud gets a joke answer | Moderation queue on; or embrace one laugh and move on |
