LEGAL SIGNABILITY CHECKLIST — Payer-Provider Contract Instruments (2026-2030 Wargame, White Cell adjudication standard)

**Rule of thumb: outcomes-based UM terms fail; process/transparency terms survive; anything binding the self-funded book must run through the ASO contract, not state law.**

---

**NOT SIGNABLE (strike or convert)**

1. **Numeric denial-rate corridors / denial-rate floors or targets.** NOT SIGNABLE. State UM laws prohibit compensating or incentivizing reviewers based on denial volume (Cal. Health & Safety Code §1367.01 / Cal. Lab. Code §4610(f): no "financial incentive or consideration... based on the number of modifications or denials"; NCQA/URAC UM accreditation "affirmative statement" standards, incorporated in most plan contracts, prohibit denial quotas). A contractual corridor also commits the plan to non-individualized outcomes, conflicting with statutes requiring case-by-case medical-necessity determination by licensed clinicians (and, for self-funded books, ERISA fiduciary duty of individualized claims adjudication, 29 C.F.R. §2560.503-1). *Signable conversion:* denial-rate **reporting and trigger** terms — payer reports rates by code family; breach of a threshold triggers audit rights, joint review, or gold-card expansion, never a duty to approve/deny N%.

2. **Provider-side collective negotiation of denial/payment terms by non-integrated competitors.** NOT SIGNABLE. Per se Sherman Act §1 price-fixing/boycott exposure; no clinical/financial integration defense; the 1996/2011 DOJ-FTC healthcare safety zones (including the messenger-model and info-exchange zones) were withdrawn Feb/Jul 2023, so no safe harbor remains — pure case-by-case rule of reason at best.

3. **Exclusive delegation + data-capture clauses** (payer gets exclusive rights to provider clinical/claims data, or provider is sole delegated UM entity with anti-steering/anti-tiering protections). NOT SIGNABLE AS DRAFTED. Exclusive dealing and steering restraints have drawn §1/§2 consent decrees (U.S. v. United Regional (2011); U.S. v. Atrium Health anti-steering decree (2019)); MFN clauses restricted in ~20 states after U.S. v. BCBS Michigan; exclusive data lockups by providers risk 21st Century Cures information-blocking liability. *Conversion:* non-exclusive delegation, revocable on notice, with mutual non-exclusive data licenses and CMS-style downstream-entity terms (42 C.F.R. §422.504(i): revocation rights, audit access, compliance flow-downs).

---

**CONVERTIBLE (White Cell converts to process form)**

4. **Pooled denial observatory (multi-provider or payer-provider data pool).** CONVERTIBLE. Raw exchange of competitively sensitive contracting data among competitors is §1 exposure post-2023 withdrawal. *Conversion:* independent third-party manager; aggregated, de-identified, historical (3+ months) outputs; no participant sees rivals' disaggregated data; no discussion of rates, contracting strategy, or collective payer boycott; outputs published to regulators/public. (Old safety-zone criteria survive as persuasive risk-mitigation, not immunity.)

5. **Joint payer-provider standards board (shared rule layer for edits/prior-auth logic).** CONVERTIBLE — defensible if structured as a legitimate SDO: open membership, published procedures, appeal rights, no price/output/reimbursement terms, voluntary adoption. Capture of the process creates liability (Allied Tube v. Indian Head (1988); ASME v. Hydrolevel (1982)); registration under the Standards Development Organization Advancement Act of 2004 secures rule-of-reason treatment and detrebling. Stronger: a state-chartered board with clear legislative articulation **and active state supervision** (Parker v. Brown; N.C. Dental v. FTC — active supervision is mandatory, not optional).

---

**SIGNABLE AS-IS**

6. **Machine-readable edit-library disclosure.** SIGNABLE. Extends existing state prompt-pay/clean-claim disclosure duties (e.g., Tex. Ins. Code ch. 1301 disclosure of claims-payment procedures and coding edits) and CMS-0057-F prior-auth transparency norms; pure unilateral disclosure, no antitrust issue.

7. **Rule-change notice periods.** SIGNABLE. Many states already mandate notice of material changes to provider contracts/edit rules — e.g., Cal. Health & Safety Code §1375.7 (45 business days for material contract changes); Texas 90-day notice of claims-payment procedure changes — so a contractual 60-90 day notice term merely codifies/extends statute.

8. **Audit rights (bilateral: provider audits payer edit engine; payer audits delegated function).** SIGNABLE. Ordinary contract law; mirrors mandatory CMS downstream-audit terms; cap look-back and scope symmetrically to match state recoupment-limit statutes.

9. **Gold-carding criteria.** SIGNABLE. Statutorily blessed model (Tex. Ins. Code ch. 4201 subch. N, per HB 3459 (2021)); contractual gold-carding can be broader than statute. Wargame note: TDI data show only ~3% of Texas providers had qualified as of Dec. 2024, and the statute reaches only the ~20% state-regulated market — so contractual gold-carding is where the leverage is.

10. **Model-change disclosure (algorithm/AI version, training-data vintage, threshold changes in UM models).** SIGNABLE. Aligned with state AI-UM statutes (Cal. SB 1120 (2024): physician review, no denial by algorithm alone) and CMS MA rules limiting algorithmic denial; disclosure duties are enforceable process terms.

11. **External-review terms (expanded scope, shorter clocks, binding effect).** SIGNABLE. Floor already exists — ACA §2719 external review binds even self-funded non-grandfathered plans (federal IRO process); contracts may expand scope/speed above the floor.

---

**ERISA/STATE-LAW SPLIT (applies to every instrument above)**
State UM, prompt-pay, gold-card, and AI statutes do **not** reach self-funded plans (ERISA §514 deemer clause, FMC v. Holliday); Rutledge v. PCMA (2020) opens only rate-like TPA regulation. **Instruments bind the self-funded book only as contract terms signed by the ASO/TPA applying network-wide** — so every checklist item must state "applies to all lines of business administered on this network, including ASO," or the White Cell scores it as fully-insured-only (~20-50% of covered lives). Federal floors that do bind self-funded books: ERISA claims regulation (29 C.F.R. §2560.503-1), ACA §2719 external review, No Surprises Act.
