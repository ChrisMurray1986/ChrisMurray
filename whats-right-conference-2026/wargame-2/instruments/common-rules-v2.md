COMMON RULES v2 — "PAYER vs PROVIDER AI, 2026–2030" (Wargame 2.0)

**0. PREMISE & BASELINE.** US claims adjudication is entering an open contest of automation: payers scaling AI payment integrity and utilization review, providers scaling AI documentation and denial defense, employers and vendors monetizing both sides. 2026 starting state: initial denials above 10% for a large minority of providers; clinical-denial overturns commonly 55–65% on appeal; GenAI rev-cycle deployment concentrated in large systems; thin hospital operating margins; CMS-0057-F prior-auth APIs binding January 2027; state AI-UR legislation and algorithmic-denial litigation in motion. Default trajectory absent effective play: adverse actions drift upward, adoption stays size-bifurcated, most pilots never reach production.

**1. PLAYER CELLS (five, all first-class).**

**BLUE — provider coalition** (moves must name the acting archetype):
- *Meridian* ($9B multi-state AMC): can self-build agentic appeals, contracts-as-code, autonomy ladders; slowed by committees and 12–24 mo IT cycles.
- *Lakeview* ($1.6B regional; one payer = 45% of commercial book): leverage only at renewal; retaliation is existential.
- *Prairie* ($380M rural; no analytics staff): buy-only — vendors, coalitions, shared services.
- *Summit* (large physician enterprise): agile, capital-light, exposed to prior-auth burden and steering.

**RED — payer coalition**, each archetype now carrying an explicit P&L state sheet updated every turn — ASO vs fully-insured mix, payment-integrity fee revenue (fees earned recovering employer money on ASO business are Red REVENUE, not savings), MLR position by segment, Stars-linked revenue at risk, and an employer-retention index:
- *NationalCo* (diversified national with services arm): frontier automation; large ASO book means PI fees are a profit center; litigation and optics constraints.
- *RegionalMutual* (dominant Blues-type): market power over Lakeview/Prairie; home-market reputation exposure; slower stack.
- *GovProgramCo* (Medicaid/MA): strongest automation incentive, highest CMS scrutiny, Stars and audit exposure.

**GREEN — self-funded employer coalition** (new). Objectives: lower net PMPM spend, fiduciary defensibility under CAA, benefits satisfaction. Move types: RFP terms conditioning ASO awards (audit rights, PI-fee caps, denial-data access); direct contracting with Blue archetypes; hiring or firing PI vendors independent of the carrier; CAA fiduciary-duty litigation posture against carriers; coalition purchasing standards. Green is the money in the ASO system — Red's PI fee revenue and retention index respond directly to Green's moves.

**VENDOR — PE-backed duopoly playing both sides** (new). One holding entity operating (a) a payment-integrity vendor selling to Red and Green and (b) a denial-defense/appeals-automation vendor selling to Blue. Its business model *is* the conflict. Objectives: ARR growth, take-rate on disputed dollars, exit multiple by 2030. Move types: product launches and pricing on either side; contingency-fee vs SaaS structuring; lobbying against denial-rate corridors and any instrument that shrinks disputed volume; talent raids on payer SIU and provider rev-cycle teams; selective data-sharing offers. White adjudicates Vendor conflicts of interest as discoverable facts other cells can exploit.

**WHITE CELL** controls CMS, states, courts, media, patient-side appeal AI (exogenous growth each turn), labor market — and runs the three procedural ledgers in §4.

**2. VICTORY CONDITIONS — own economics only.** Each side is scored solely on its own book. There is no shared-loss outcome and no outcome-protected actor: a Prairie closure, a Lakeview distress sale, a GovProgramCo contract loss are *measured events* that flow through the relevant side's metrics, never scoring events in themselves.

**BLUE wins** if by end-2030, in ordinal terms (§3.6): (a) net-revenue leakage % (denials + underpayments + timely-filing write-offs) shows a moderate-or-large decline coalition-wide; (b) total adverse-action cost — internal rework, vendor fees, appeal labor — as a share of net patient revenue declines or holds while volume of disputes rises; (c) archetype operating margins: at least three of four improve or hold, with no archetype scored on survival per se.

**RED wins** if by end-2030: (a) PMPM medical-cost trend holds at or below its book-of-business benchmark; (b) payment-integrity yield holds or grows, *counting ASO fee revenue as revenue net of Green fee-cap and clawback actions*; (c) Stars revenue at risk does not materially convert to losses; (d) employer-retention index holds — ASO accounts lost to Green direct contracting or rival carriers count against Red regardless of medical-cost performance.

GREEN and VENDOR receive their own end-state ordinal ranks (Green: net spend trend, fiduciary exposure, member friction; Vendor: ARR, take-rate, exit multiple) and can "win" alongside either side — the adjudicator's final report ranks all four player cells.

**3. ADJUDICATION DOCTRINE (retained core + new gates).**

3.1 *Base-rate anchor.* Every ruling cites the evidence brief. Unmanaged drift: adverse actions rise ~10–20% per 18-month turn; pilot-purgatory persists; small actors cannot self-build.

3.2 *Friction.* Contract terms bind only at renewal (1–3 yr cycles; earliest broad effect Turn 2). IT/AI deployments take 12–24 months to measurable impact. Scale claims from pilot-stage actors are struck.

3.3 *Bounded movement.* Per turn, absent a White-declared structural break: denial rates ±2 pts; overturn rates ±10 pts; cost-per-touch ±35%; FTE reduction ≤40% of a function. Overreaching move sheets are cut to bounds and the actor's next turn is adjudicated skeptically.

3.4 *Second-order effects, mandatory.* Litigation and AG exposure; media and political salience (feeds the regulatory dial); patient-AI appeal volume responding to denial levels; workforce effects (redeployment credible, mass cuts risk error spikes and coverage).

3.5 *Simultaneity.* Moves resolve concurrently. Symmetric escalation cancels on headline metrics while inflating both sides' admin cost — recorded honestly in each side's own economics (Vendor typically profits). Asymmetric, cooperative, or Green-brokered moves are the main route to large shifts.

3.6 *Ordinal outputs.* All metric changes reported as direction + magnitude band (small / moderate / large); end-state results as ordinal ranks. White may not invent precise dollar figures; any specific number must trace to the evidence brief.

3.7 *No narrative reuse.* All environment updates and rulings written fresh each turn; no recycled phrasing from prior studies or turns.

**4. WHITE CELL PROCEDURES — run every turn, in order.**

**(A) Legal-signability gate.** Before any contract instrument takes effect, White tests it against a signability checklist: Does it function as a denial quota or corridor prohibited or preempted under state UM/insurance law? Does it require data sharing barred by HIPAA/antitrust? Would competent counsel on either side sign it? Non-signable instruments (e.g., numeric denial-rate corridors) are struck or converted to signable process terms (audit rights, escalation SLAs, gold-carding criteria, transparency reporting), with the conversion recorded.

**(B) Market-diffusion ledger.** For every multilateral instrument (shared rule layers, contracts-as-code standards, pre-adjudication protocols), White records adoption state: signed by table actors → piloted → adopted by early external movers → industry infrastructure. Diffusion is adjudicated against the base-rate table: standards bodies take 15+ years to reach ubiquity; prior interoperability frameworks diffused thin over 7+ years; state gold-carding uptake ran ~3%. Signature by the seven table actors is *not* industry infrastructure; instruments confer only signatory-level benefits until diffusion clears realistic thresholds White states in advance.

**(C) Institutional milestone ledger.** Every structural initiative gets a milestone track with lag table: standards formation 10–15 yrs; statute enactment-to-operationalization 3–7 yrs; novel contract-language diffusion 3–7 yrs; IT deployment 12–24 mo; contract repricing 1–3 yrs. Each turn White advances or stalls each track with reasons. Instruments that cannot mathematically mature by 2030 are scored at game end as immature — partial credit for milestones reached, no credit for projected effects. That is the honest outcome.

**5. TURN STRUCTURE.** Three turns: **T1 2026–27** (CMS-0057-F lands; state bills reach floors; positioning, procurement, RFP cycles, contract setup), **T2 2028** (renewed contracts bind; T1 investments pay or fail; T1 litigation reaches rulings; first Stars/MLR consequences; Green RFP awards land), **T3 2029–30** (mature effects or declared failures; final scoring). Each turn: (1) White environment update + three-ledger report; (2) simultaneous secret move sheets from all four player cells (objective, 2–3 concrete actions, resources, assumptions, acting archetype); (3) signability screening of any instruments; (4) adjudication with ordinal metric deltas and second-order ledger; (5) public narrative update.

**6. SCOREBOARD (per archetype/cell, per turn, ordinal bands).** Blue: initial denial rate, overturn rate, adverse-action cost share of NPR, net leakage %, margin, litigation/reputation (1–5). Red: PMPM trend vs benchmark, PI yield (fees as revenue), auto-adjudication share, MLR by segment, Stars at risk, employer-retention index, litigation/reputation (1–5). Green: net spend trend, fiduciary exposure, member friction. Vendor: ARR by side, take-rate, lobbying wins, conflict-exposure flag.
