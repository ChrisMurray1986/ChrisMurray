EVIDENCE BRIEF A — PROVIDER-SIDE AND NEUTRAL SURVEY BASE RATES (2024–2026)
Sourcing constraint honored: all figures below come from provider-side or neutral survey/benchmark/academic sources. No payer earnings calls or payer trade material used.

1. DENIAL LEVELS AND TRENDS — DENIALS ARE STILL RISING, MODESTLY BUT MONOTONICALLY
- Average initial denial rate across ~2,300 hospitals: 11.6% of claims in 2025, up from 11.4% in 2024; median final denial rate rose 2.5% → 2.7%, with clinical denials (prior auth/precert and medical necessity) driving virtually all of the increase (Kodiak Solutions benchmarking, March 2026).
- Net revenue lost to final denials plus patient bad debt: >$48B in 2025, up 25% from $38.6B in 2024; median bad debt rate 1.1% → 1.3% (Kodiak, 2026).
- Share of providers reporting denial rates of 10%+: 30% (2022) → 38% (2024) → 41% (2025); 54% say claim errors are increasing and 68% say clean-claim submission is harder than a year ago (Experian Health State of Claims, 2024 and 2025).
- Nearly half of revenue-cycle leaders now name payer denials the single greatest threat to financial performance (HFMA/Knowtion survey of 172 executives, 2025).
- Benchmark MA behavior: MA plans denied 17% of initial claims (16.6% dollar-weighted); only 60% of denials were resubmitted; net effect was a 7% reduction in provider MA revenue even after overturns (Vabson, Hicks, Chernew, Health Affairs, 2025).

2. APPEAL OVERTURN RATES — HIGH WHEN FOUGHT, BUT FALLING
- Median provider appeal success dropped from 56% to 45% for private plans and 51% to 41% for Medicaid over two years (HFMA/Knowtion, 2025). Kodiak (2026) independently reports a slight decline in initial-denial overturn rates in 2025.
- Health Affairs (2025): 57% of MA denials ultimately overturned; two-thirds of resubmitted denials succeed — yet 40% of denials are never resubmitted. The asymmetry (appeals usually win, most denials aren't appealed) is the core economics of the game.
- Cost of fighting: hospitals spent ~$18B overturning denials in 2025 within ~$43B total spent trying to collect payment insurers owe for delivered care (AHA Cost of Caring, 2026); rework runs ~$57/claim, ~$19.7B/year (Premier, 2024).

3. PROVIDER AI ADOPTION — STRATEGY IS UNIVERSAL, DEPLOYMENT IS THIN
- ~70% of providers have an AI strategy in place or in development; ambient documentation is furthest along (~1 in 5 at full rollout, ~2 in 5 piloting); top RCM use cases are ambient documentation, CDI, coding, and prior auth; <5% of providers say deployed AI missed expectations (Bain/KLAS survey of 228 US healthcare executives, Oct 2025).
- But in claims operations specifically, only 14% of providers have implemented AI even though 67% believe it would improve claims — and 62% now claim AI fluency (up from 28% in 2024). Stall points: accuracy, HIPAA compliance, staff training, and doubt that AI can track payer-specific rules (Experian State of Claims, 2025). Of the adopter minority, 69% report reduced denials or better resubmission success.
- 63% of organizations report using some AI/automation in the revenue cycle broadly (HFMA/Eliciting Insights survey of 272 executives, 2024). Read: high automation, shallow true-AI penetration; pilots concentrate where ROI is hard-dollar (Bain/KLAS, 2025).

4. COST-TO-COLLECT AND WORKFORCE
- Best-practice cost-to-collect benchmark has tightened from ~3% to ~2% of net patient revenue; >4% is a distress signal; small providers run ~3%, large systems <1.5% (HFMA MAP Keys guidance, 2025).
- Average hospital employed ~64 dedicated administrative/billing FTEs in 2024 — about 6.5% of total hospital employment (AHA, 2026).
- Persistent shortages of experienced coders, billers, and A/R specialists push leaders toward automation, outsourcing, and global staffing; AI is already displacing routine coding/documentation work toward exception management (HFMA revenue-cycle workforce reporting, 2025–2026; Guidehouse/HFMA RCM Trends, 2026).

5. PRIOR AUTHORIZATION BURDEN
- Physicians average ~40 prior auths/week, consuming ~13 hours of physician+staff time; 95% say PA delays necessary care; 79% report treatment abandonment; 32% say requests are often/always denied; ~1 in 4 report a serious adverse event tied to PA (AMA Prior Authorization Survey, fielded Dec 2025, released 2026 — physicians report the June 2025 industry "reform pledge" has not changed frontline experience).

6. PATIENT-SIDE AI APPEAL TOOLS (NEW ACTOR, 2024–2026)
- Consumer tools now generate appeals directly: Counterforce Health (free) claims ~70%+ overturn success; Claimable ($50/appeal, launched Oct 2024) reports ~85% success in targeted high-denial conditions (local/press coverage, 2025). Historically only ~1% of patients appealed; these tools attack that non-appeal gap — a demand-side shock neither payer nor provider fully controls.

7. PROVIDER MARGINS — THIN CUSHION FOR ESCALATION
- Median hospital operating margin ~1.3% calendar-YTD through December 2025, with 2026 framed as a "new normal": expenses up ~7.5% in 2025 (growing roughly twice as fast as prices), payer mix shifting, bad debt/charity care rising (Kaufman Hall National Hospital Flash Report, Dec 2025/Jan 2026; AHA, 2026).

KEY GAME DYNAMICS THESE DATA IMPLY: denials rise ~0.2pp/yr while overturn rates fall ~5pp/yr; providers win most fights they pick but can't afford to pick them all; provider AI is 1–2 years behind in claims ops with a 14% deployment base; margins near 1% mean small denial shifts are existential.

GAME DISCIPLINE: adjudicate against these base rates.
