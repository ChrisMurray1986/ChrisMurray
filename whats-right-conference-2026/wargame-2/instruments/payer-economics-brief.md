PAYER ECONOMICS BRIEF — Red Team (Payer) Playbook

**1. MLR mechanics: why $1 of denied claims isn't $1 of profit — except when it is**
ACA floors: insurers must spend ≥80% of premium on claims + quality improvement (individual/small group) or ≥85% (large group); MA and Medicaid MCOs also face 85% floors (KFF, 2025). Excess margin is rebated: insurers paid ~$1.1B in 2024 rebates and ~$1.6B in 2025 (avg $192/person), computed on a rolling 3-year average — ~$14.3B rebated since 2012 (KFF, 2025; healthinsurance.org, 2025).
Game implication: on **fully-insured** books, aggressive denials/payment-integrity (PI) recoveries lower the claims numerator; if MLR falls below the floor, savings partially leak back as rebates, and lower claims compress next year's allowable premium base (the "15-20% of a bigger number" incentive to let trend run). On **ASO/self-funded** books, MLR rules don't apply at all — claim dollars are the employer's money, so the payer monetizes cost control only through **fees**. Critics document insurers reclassifying admin costs as QI to skirt MLR (AHCJ, 2025). Recoveries on fully-insured reduce incurred claims in the MLR numerator; on ASO they flow to the employer, but the payer keeps a contingency cut.

**2. ASO economics: PI is a fee-revenue growth engine**
63% of covered workers are in self-funded plans (79% at large firms) — down slightly from 65% in 2023 (KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey, 2024). ASO revenue = admin fees (TPA fees roughly $5-60 PEPM by group size; network-access fees $6-35 PEPM; medical-management carve-outs $5-15 PEPM) plus **variable "shared savings" fees**: PI vendors and payer services arms typically charge contingency fees around 20% of recoveries, with out-of-network "savings" fees historically similar (MultiPlan model) (healthtechstack.io, 2024; ClarisHealth, 2024).
Key asymmetry: every dollar of recovered/denied provider payment on ASO generates ~$0.20 of nearly pure-margin fee revenue for the payer, at zero claims-cost benefit to the payer itself. This is why PI is a growth business: Optum Insight ran ~$18.8B revenue in 2024 and returned to +6% growth in Q2 2025; Carelon Services grew 36% YoY in Q2 2025; Cotiviti markets PI explicitly as a counter to rising MLRs (UnitedHealth/Elevance filings, 2024-25; Cotiviti, 2025). Red team should treat PI escalation (itemized-bill review, DRG downgrades, level-of-care audits) as revenue expansion, not just cost control — and note vertical integration means Optum/Carelon audit claims their parent pays.

**3. Stars/MA: quality bonuses are the biggest single lever**
MA quality bonus payments total ≥$12.7B in 2025 and >$13B in 2026; 75% of MA enrollees are in bonus-earning (4+ star) contracts; 4+ stars brings a 5% benchmark bonus plus higher rebate retention (KFF, 2025-26). Downside is existential: Humana's 2025 Stars collapse (4+ star membership from 94% → 25% after one big contract fell 4.5 → 3.5) created an estimated $1-3B revenue hit, drove a $3.5B 2026 earnings headwind, and two failed lawsuits against CMS (Healthcare Dive, 2024-25).
UM interaction: aggressive prior auth/denials feed **CTM complaints** (complaints per 1,000 members, a Stars measure), appeals-timeliness measures, and disenrollment — historically 4x-weighted patient-experience measures. Caveat for 2026+ play: CMS's Stars overhaul removes ~11 measures including complaints, appeals, and disenrollment starting with 2029 Stars (2027 measurement year), materially **lowering the Stars penalty for UM aggressiveness** going forward (Healthcare Dive, 2026; CMS Technical Notes, 2025). Near-term, CTMs and IRE appeal overturns still bite.

**4. Employer dynamics: the abrasion constraint is real and growing**
Self-funded employers increasingly demand: trend/performance guarantees, audit rights, full claims-data access, and independent PI audits of their TPA. CAA 2021 (gag-clause ban, fiduciary transparency) armed plaintiffs: Lewandowski v. J&J (2024, drug-cost fiduciary breach — dismissed on standing, again in Nov 2025), Navarro v. Wells Fargo (dismissed on standing), and Stern v. JPMorgan (motion to dismiss partially denied March 2025; in discovery) (Georgetown Litigation Tracker; Trucker Huss, 2025). Employers, fearing their own fiduciary liability, now pressure TPAs for transparency and sue them directly (e.g., cross-plan offsetting and overpayment-recovery practices). Red-team constraint: opaque fees, denied claims that land on employees, and refusal of data access create employer abrasion — consultants move books of business; a large jumbo account loss dwarfs PI fee income on that account. But standing dismissals (J&J, Wells Fargo) mean litigation deterrence is currently weaker than headlines suggest.

**5. Prior-auth/denial economics: cheap to deny, expensive to get caught**
Cost per PA transaction: ~$3.41-3.52 manual vs ~$0.05 electronic for payers (CAQH Index, 2023-24) — automation makes review nearly free, so denial volume scales without cost. Appeals asymmetry: only a small share of denials are appealed, so even high overturn rates (plaintiffs allege ~90% of appealed nH Predict denials reversed) are cheap in expectation.
Litigation exposure — real but survivable so far:
- **nH Predict (UnitedHealth/naviHealth)**: Estate of Lokken class action survived dismissal in Feb 2025 on breach-of-contract/good-faith theories (most state claims preempted by Medicare Act); court ordered broad discovery into AI-driven post-acute denials in 2026 (DLA Piper, 2025; Becker's, 2026). Risk vector: discovery of internal targets tying algorithm to length-of-stay cuts.
- **PXDX (Cigna)**: ProPublica exposé — one doctor denying up to 60,000 claims/month, 1.2 sec/claim without opening files. March 2025: ERISA fiduciary-breach claim allowed to proceed; benefit-denial claims dismissed; some plaintiffs lost standing (Courthouse News, 2025). Actual consequences to date: litigation costs, CA regulator scrutiny, reputational damage — no major fine yet.
Net payer calculus: batch/algorithmic review remains ROI-positive on pure economics; the binding constraints are Stars/CTM exposure (MA), state AI-in-UM laws, CMS's 2024 rule requiring individualized MA medical-necessity determinations, and post-Thompson-shooting political salience — not, so far, adverse judgments.

**Red-team incentive summary**: Maximize UM/PI aggression on ASO (fee revenue, no MLR drag, employer is the payer); moderate on fully-insured (MLR rebate leakage); protect Stars on MA near-term but expect the complaints/appeals measures to vanish by 2029; treat litigation as a cost of doing business unless discovery exposes explicit denial targets; watch employer abrasion as the true strategic constraint.
