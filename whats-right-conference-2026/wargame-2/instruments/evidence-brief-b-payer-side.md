EVIDENCE BRIEF B — PAYER-SIDE, LEGAL, AND MARKET BASE RATES (2024–2026)

1. PAYER AI STRATEGY AND SPEND

- UnitedHealth Group is spending ~$1.5B on AI in 2026, roughly one-third funding software products/platforms as Optum Insight pivots to an "AI-first services business"; management guided to a conservative 2:1 return with 12–18 month paybacks on many programs (UNH earnings call via Becker's Payer Issues / PYMNTS, 2026).
- Optum launched "Optum Real," billed as the industry's first real-time claims/reimbursement platform — 500M transactions processed in-year, targeted to 2.5B by year-end — plus "Optum Integrity One" AI auto-coding (UNH earnings materials, 2025–26).
- Centene told investors it scores claims against a library of 75 payment-integrity algorithms and is deploying AI in prior-auth review and call centers, framing data/AI as a core margin-recovery lever after a difficult 2025 (Centene Q4 2025 earnings call, Feb 2026).
- Elevance cites advanced analytics for FWA detection; Carelon hit $71.7B revenue in 2025 (+33% YoY), with Carelon Services growing revenue and operating gain >50% via risk-based deals (Elevance Q4 2025 release/earnings call, 2026).
- Cigna is weighing "strategic alternatives" — sale, partnership, or combination — for EviCore, its prior-auth/UM subsidiary (Becker's Payer, 2025–26): even payers see standalone UM as strategically awkward under current scrutiny.

2. PRIOR-AUTH RETREAT COMMITMENTS (REAL BUT PARTIAL)

- June 2025 AHIP/BCBSA pledge: 48 insurers (UHC, Aetna, Cigna, Humana, 30+ Blues) committed to standardized FHIR ePA by Jan 1, 2027, with ≥80% of clean electronic PA requests answered in real time in 2027, reduced PA scope, and continuity-of-care protections (AHIP, 2025). Progress claim: insurers report ~11% of prior authorizations eliminated under the pledge (Fierce Healthcare, 2026).
- UnitedHealthcare cut requirements covering ~10% of PA volume in 2025 and committed to eliminating 30% of current PA volume by end-2026 (outpatient surgeries, echocardiograms, therapies) (UHG newsroom, 2026). Its Gold Card program grew qualifying provider groups >40% in 2025 and cut PA volume ~30% for eligible groups (UHC newsroom, 2025).
- Centene is removing PA on select codes in phases — ~300,000 authorizations/year eliminated from imaging alone starting Q4 2025 (Centene, 2025).
- Countervailing structure: ~11 states (TX, AR, CO, LA, WV, WY, IL, MI, NM, VT, +) have gold-card statutes, but payer-side analysts and NAIC note weak uptake and narrow eligibility; 2025 sessions mostly amended rather than expanded (Aimed Alliance Gold Card Analysis, 2025; NAIC PA white paper, Dec 2025).

3. THE ECONOMIC ENGINE: MA MARGIN COMPRESSION

- MA margins compressed through 2025 on elevated senior utilization, V28 risk-model phase-in, and rate pressure; 2026 saw one of the highest plan-exit/service-area-reduction years on record (UHC, Humana, Aetna county exits) (HealthScape/PwC MA analyses, 2025–26).
- 2026 Star averages flat at 3.66 vs 3.65; quality-bonus payments per member fell ~10% (~$40) from 2023–2025 (CMS 2026 Stars via Healthscape/Chartis, 2025). Implication for the game: payers under MA margin pressure have strong incentive to intensify payment integrity and UM even while publicly loosening PA.

4. LITIGATION LANDSCAPE — AI-UR IS DISCOVERABLE AND SURVIVING DISMISSAL

- Estate of Lokken v. UnitedHealth (nH Predict, alleged ~90% error rate on post-acute denials): Feb 13, 2025 order let breach-of-contract and good-faith claims proceed; a March 2025 order compelled broad discovery into UHC's AI claims processes (D. Minn.; Georgetown Health Care Litigation Tracker; ArentFox Schiff, 2025).
- Kisting-Leung v. Cigna (PXDX): March 31, 2025 mixed ruling — some plaintiffs lacked standing and benefit-denial claims were dismissed with leave to amend, but ERISA fiduciary-breach claims over algorithmic review without medical-director evaluation proceed (E.D. Cal., 2025; Georgetown tracker).
- Net: courts are not blocking AI use per se, but "human decision-maker" plan language and ERISA fiduciary duties are live theories, and algorithms are subject to court-ordered disclosure.

5. LEGISLATION — HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP MANDATES SPREADING

- California SB 1120 (effective Jan 1, 2025): AI/algorithms cannot deny, delay, or modify care on medical-necessity grounds; only a licensed clinician may make that determination; decisions must use individual clinical circumstances, not solely group data; DMHC/DOI audit authority (CA statute, 2024; Chambers Healthcare AI Guide, 2025). Colorado's SB 24-205 classes healthcare-access AI as "high-risk" (2024). Multiple states copied SB 1120-style bills in 2025 sessions (Hooper Lundy; Maynard Nexsen trackers, 2025).

6. VENDOR MARKET — PE CAPITAL IS FLOODING PAYMENT INTEGRITY

- KKR/Veritas recapitalized Cotiviti at ~$11B (closed May 2024); Cotiviti then bought Edifecs for ~$3B (closed March 2025), taking on ~$7B total debt (PitchBook; Cotiviti releases, 2024–25).
- New Mountain Capital merged Rawlings Group + Apixio PI + Varis, then acquired Machinify (Jan 2025) to form a >$500M-revenue AI payment-integrity platform positioned against Optum and Cotiviti; Machinify then bought Performant Healthcare for ~$670M (2025) (Fierce Healthcare; Machinify, 2025).
- Market analysts size healthcare payment integrity at roughly $9–15B (2025) growing 13–15% CAGR, with a decisive shift from post-pay audit to pre-pay/pre-claim prevention (pre-pay ~43% share, 2024) (Mordor Intelligence; Precedence Research, 2025).

7. HARD BASE RATES FOR ADJUDICATION

- MA insurers made ~53M PA determinations in 2024; only a small share of denials are appealed (1.6%–19.9% by insurer), but 80.7% of appeals were partially/fully overturned in 2024 (95.5% at Centene) (KFF analysis of payers' CMS filings, 2025).
