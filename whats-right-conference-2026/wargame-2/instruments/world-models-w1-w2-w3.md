WHITE CELL WORLD-MODEL ENSEMBLE — Wargame 2.0 (2026-2030, 1 turn = ~6 months, 8-10 turns)

Common rules: White Cell declares active world at T0; players are NOT told which. Environment updates are delivered as news/regulatory bulletins each turn. Dice/randomizer resolves frequency guidance ("1-in-3" = 33% per turn).

---

**W1 — ACTIVIST** (fast, pro-oversight environment)

- **Statutes:** Any state AI-UR bill introduced in a player-relevant state passes within 1 turn and is operational (enforcement, audit rights, physician-review mandates) within 1-2 turns. By T4, 15+ states have live regimes. Private rights of action included in ~half.
- **Courts:** Denial/AI-UR suits reach preliminary rulings in 1-2 turns; settlements arrive fast and include PROCESS terms (algorithm audits, override logs, external review). Class certification granted more often than base rate. Injunctions against automated-denial pipelines are plausible from T2.
- **CMS:** 0057-F enforced strictly Jan 2027 (T2): no extensions, corrective action plans and Stars/penalty exposure for non-compliant payers. CMS issues sub-regulatory guidance every 1-2 turns tightening prior-auth turnaround and transparency.
- **Media:** Every player denial-scandal or leaked metric becomes a multi-turn national story; amplification biased against payers. Provider missteps get 1-turn coverage, payer missteps 2-3 turns.
- **Patient-AI:** Appeal-bot adoption doubles per turn from a 2% base; by T6, 25-35% of adverse determinations are contested with AI assistance. Success rates rise as tools cite state statutes.
- **Employers (GREEN passive):** Employers respond to media cycles — after any major payer scandal, 1-2 large employers per turn demand audit rights or switch carriers/TPAs.
- **FTC/AGs:** Actively midwife structures — offer consent-decree templates, safe harbors for provider data-sharing collaboratives, and pre-clearance for joint UR standards.
- **Event frequency:** 1 regulatory action/turn guaranteed; 1-in-2 chance of a court ruling with process terms; 1 media event/turn.
- **Example updates:** (1) T2 bulletin: "CMS denies all 0057-F extension requests; 4 national payers receive compliance notices; stock dips 3-6%." (2) T3: "CA AG announces consent decree with [payer]: mandatory physician review of all AI-flagged denials, quarterly public audit — template offered to other payers."

---

**W2 — SLOW/PASSIVE** (base-rate calibrated; the null-hypothesis world)

- **Statutes:** State AI-UR laws pass in only 4-8 states over the whole game; passage-to-operational-effect lag is 3-7 years, so almost nothing bites before T6-T8. Rulemaking stalls; enforcement budgets are thin; first enforcement actions are single-digit fines. Federal posture 2026-2028 is deregulation-leaning: no national AI-UR standard, agencies signal forbearance.
- **Courts:** 3-5 years to merits (i.e., beyond game horizon for suits filed after T2). 85-90% settle quietly, confidential, cash-only, NO process commitments. No precedent accumulates. Class certification usually denied.
- **CMS:** Grants 0057-F extensions and enforcement discretion through 2028 (T4-T5); compliance becomes attestation-driven. Real teeth, if any, arrive T7+.
- **Media:** Episodic — a denial story flares for 1 turn then dies unless a fresh shock renews it. No sustained campaign. Coverage roughly symmetric payer/provider.
- **Patient-AI:** Grows but stays niche: 2% → 8-10% of appeals by game end, concentrated among high-literacy/commercially insured patients. No statutory tailwind.
- **Employers (GREEN passive):** Inertial. Renew incumbent carriers absent >8% premium shock; audit-rights demands rare (1-in-4 turns, one employer).
- **FTC/AGs:** Reactive only; open occasional inquiries that close without action.
- **Event frequency:** Regulatory action 1-in-3 turns (usually symbolic); consequential court event 1-in-4 turns; media flare 1-in-2 turns, decays next turn; patient-AI milestone every 2-3 turns.
- **Example updates:** (1) T3: "CMS extends 0057-F enforcement discretion 18 months for plans showing 'good-faith implementation'; trade press covers it; general media does not." (2) T5: "Class action over AI denials settles for $18M, no admission, no process changes; terms sealed. Two copycat suits filed, expected to resolve similarly."

---

**W3 — HOSTILE/CHAOTIC** (adversarial, high-variance environment)

- **Statutes:** State AI-UR laws pass sporadically, but federal preemption threat (executive order or appropriations rider) freezes operationalization: any state regime has a 1-in-3 chance per turn of being stayed or chilled. Compliance planning is whipsawed.
- **Courts:** Outcomes drawn effectively at random: same fact pattern can yield dismissal, a punitive verdict, or a bizarre procedural result; venue matters more than merits. No stable precedent. Settlements unpredictable in size and terms.
- **CMS:** Budget-driven and inconsistent — alternates between enforcement sweeps (when politically useful) and mass extensions (when understaffed). Roll each turn: 1-in-3 strict, 1-in-3 lax, 1-in-3 silent/contradictory guidance.
- **Media:** Cuts BOTH ways: provider upcoding/AI-documentation-inflation stories run as often as payer denial stories. Any player's AI use is a liability when spotlighted. 1 hostile story/turn, target alternates or randomized.
- **Shock (mandatory):** Between T4-T6, a major data breach or AI scandal (misdiagnosis at scale, leaked training data, or a payer-algorithm exposé) craters public trust in ALL health-AI; patient-AI adoption drops 50% for 2 turns, then rebounds angrier (litigation-oriented bots).
- **Patient-AI:** Volatile — grows to ~15%, collapses at shock, returns weaponized (bots that auto-file complaints with AGs and press).
- **Employers (GREEN passive):** Skittish; respond to whichever scandal is freshest — may abandon a provider network or a carrier with equal probability.
- **Antitrust asymmetry:** FTC/DOJ block provider coordination (CINs, joint UR-response consortia get second requests or suits) while payer consolidation clears. Provider collective strategies carry regulatory risk each turn (1-in-3 investigation).
- **Event frequency:** 2 environment events/turn minimum; at least 1 must be adverse to whichever team is currently winning (White Cell discretion, disclosed post-game).
- **Example updates:** (1) T3: "DOJ files suit to block 12-hospital AI-denial-response consortium as buyer-side coordination; same week, [MegaPayer]-[Insurer] merger clears with light conditions." (2) T5 shock: "Breach exposes 40M patients' UR records including AI denial-score rationales; Congress holds hearings targeting payers AND provider upcoding; all health-AI trust polling drops 30 points."

---

**Cross-world design note:** Strategies should be stress-tested in all three; a move is robust only if it survives W2's molasses (no external forcing function rescues it) and W3's whipsaw (no dependence on stable regulatory or reputational conditions). W1 replicates the prior study's optimism and serves as the upper bound, not the base case.
