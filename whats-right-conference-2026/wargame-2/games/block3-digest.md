# Block 3: D3 asymmetric-friction x W1/W3 extremes

## B3G1 — Arms Race Unbound (D3 × W1, brief A)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** Priced Peace on Organized Books, Shadow War on the Recovery Ledger

By end-2030 the board has settled into a two-tier equilibrium organized around a single variable: whether the money funding a book of business got organized. On the organized tier — anchor jumbo employers under Green's coalition standards, the Meridian and Lakeview systems under bound riders and a litigation settlement, MA under active CMS supervision — the adversarial denial economy has been contractually retired and replaced with priced, papered cooperation: gold-carding on named lines, real-time adjudication rails, override logs and third-party algorithm audits, clawback-backed fee mechanics, and restitution settlements. This peace was never conceded; every increment of de-escalation was purchased with retention, fee revenue, term extensions, or dismissal of claims. On the unorganized tier — mid-market ASO accounts not yet gated into the purchasing pool, and above all the provider-facing recovery ledger (COB, subrogation, offsets, credit-balance reconciliation) — extraction continues at full automation, deliberately relocated to channels with no member-facing surface, no appeal right, no patient-AI trigger, and no statutory or contractual coverage. The arms race did not end; it was priced out of every venue where organized counterparties, regulators, bots, or discoverable paper could reach it, and it migrated to the one ledger none of them can see. Structural residue: a permanent employer purchasing infrastructure with consultant-embedded standards; a provider data/appeals utility past coalition scale; a compliance-SaaS layer whose demand is written into statute; a split and partially exited vendor sector; a patient-AI contest rate approaching one in six and destroying the never-appealed tail that funded the original denial game; and a fully loaded successor conflict on the recovery ledger scheduled for 2031.

**Summary:** B3G1 (doctrine D3, world W1 activist regulator, evidence brief A) opened with both automation arsenals at maximum posture and no coordination mechanisms, and closed in 2030 with a purchased, segmented peace. The governing law emerged by Turn 2 and never broke: de-escalation happens where the money watching a book of business is organized, and extraction continues where it is not. Green — the employer/ASO purchaser cell — chartered faster than any actor priced, converted CAA fiduciary process, renewal leverage, and a live direct-contract exit threat into binding fee mechanics (clawback, contingency caps, audit rights), and finished first with the game's only verified recovered dollars. Blue spent Turn 1 buying every slow clock simultaneously (litigation, coalition data trust, employer channel, legislative evidence package) and cashed them all by Turn 3: the appeals factory plus real-time telemetry manufactured NationalCo's cooperation flip conditions, and the Meridian settlement converted litigation into rider parity whose anti-rotation scope expressly named the vectors Red had rotated into. Red played segment-differentiated maximum yield, sold de-escalation at a price on organized books, and rotated three times as instruments, statutes, and patient-AI bots closed each vector — front-end edits to E/M downcodes and eligibility pends, then to itemized-bill/DRG validation and specialty pharmacy, and finally out of the denial economy entirely into COB/subrogation/credit-balance reconciliation: a channel with no member, no appeal right, no bot trigger, and no coverage under any 2026-2030 instrument. Red finished fourth — trend, Stars, and retention held, but the PI fee engine ended below its T1 peak net of clawbacks and SaaS step-downs; the fee engine, not the medical book, is where Red lost. The Vendor duopoly's dual-side conflict structure was destroyed by Green's ownership-keyed purchasing standard and litigation discovery; after a failed attestation-only separation, the holding company executed an actual split and exited on regulation-subsidized compliance rails. The W1 environment ran hot exactly as designed — 15+ AI-UR statutes, strict CMS 0057-F enforcement, an expedited docket, sustained payer-adverse media, and patient-AI contests compounding from 2% toward one in six adverse determinations — but the decisive binding constraints on Red were commercial (employer renewals, account moves, litigation pricing) rather than statutory. Every signed instrument that survived was a process or fee-mechanics term; no numeric denial-rate corridor was ever proposed in signable form. The game ends with a successor conflict fully seeded on the recovery ledger, invisible to every instrument the 2026-2030 war produced.

## B3G2 — Asymmetric Squeeze (D3 × W1, brief B)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** Evacuation Equilibrium (the denial changed clothes)

By end-2030 the war over clinical denial is finished — not because either side won it, but because the payer side evacuated the terrain once its own ledger showed the vector was the worst-priced asset it held. Six human-in-the-loop statutes (four with operating audit regimes), a class-certified algorithmic-denial settlement with batch re-review, patient-AI at 16% of contested determinations converging on the 80.7% overturn base rate, and finality-based fee clawbacks had jointly repriced medical-necessity denial to unprofitability. Red's response was neither detente nor exhaustion: it rotated its recovery engines wholesale into financial-accuracy and pricing enforcement — COB, eligibility, subrogation, duplicate payment, contract-pricing compliance, site-of-service — vectors that survive appeal, escape every clawback, and sit outside the statutes' medical-necessity scope. Adverse actions did not decline; they changed clothes. The abandoned terrain is fenced with genuinely binding paper: gold cards, notice floors, transparency schedules, process-term settlements — all real, all cheap for Red to grant because they fence what it no longer wants. The purchaser coalition holds the only functioning detection instrument in the new war — a contractual quarterly vector-disclosure schedule with fee-finality objection windows — but only on coalition paper; every non-coalition book enters 2031 inside a fresh two-turn rotation shadow. Blue's medical-necessity-trained appeal engines are mistargeted against pricing disputes, its analytics builds are dead, and patient-AI tools have no purchase on provider-facing pricing fights. The equilibrium is a displaced, re-instrumenting conflict: high transparency and heavy process obligations on the old battlefield, open shadow on the new one, with employers — not regulators — holding the binding leash on self-funded volume, and the first contested cycle of the pricing war scheduled for 2031.

**Summary:** Game B3G2 ran doctrine D3 (asymmetric friction physics: payer capability lands same-turn, provider builds run multi-turn clocks with stall/death dice) in world W1 (activist regulator, fast courts, anti-payer media) from a seed of consolidated payers pressing automated adjudication into an under-resourced provider tier. The game's arc is a three-act displacement. Act one: Red harvested uncontested yield in the shadow while Blue lit every slow fuse — litigation, statutes, data trust, employer channel — and the environment turned activist underneath Red (six HITL statutes, compelled algorithm production, a rural-hospitals media series). Act two: the dice killed Blue's measurement layer (telemetry pilot death after consecutive stalls) and pried Lakeview out of the trust, while Blue's paper tracks all advanced: direct contract live January 2028, Summit's appeals engine at enterprise scale above the 60% flip threshold, Lokken production feeding two state audits. Red, reading its own P&L, exited burned vectors behind a Fast-Pay Guarantee and — decisively — signed Green's Contract Standard v1 with clawback to hold the coalition book, installing the auditors that would document its own downcode rotation. Act three: everything matured at once. Blue harvested class certification, process-term settlements, gold-card expansions, and a favorable audited comparator year; Green got paid in every direction — clawbacks, restitution, a second carrier on the Standard, and a v1.1 vector-disclosure schedule that achieved by contract the rotation detection Blue failed to build by analytics; Vendor bought peace at a compressed multiple after Rotation Radar surfaced in discovery; and Red evacuated clinical denial entirely, swinging into COB, subrogation, eligibility, and contract-pricing recovery that no statute, appeal engine, or patient-AI tool on the board can reach. Final standings on own-book economics: Green first decisively, Red second, Blue third, Vendor fourth. The load-bearing findings: yield displaced rather than disappeared under every constraint; process terms signed everywhere while the anti-rotation clause sat unsigned for three turns (no Red ledger ever priced rotation coverage below rotation); every Red de-escalation was proximately triggered by litigation, regulation, media, or employer abrasion — never cumulative cost; the employer channel, not the activist regulator, bound Red's self-funded PI behavior first, because statutes reach medical necessity while ASO economics reach everything; and dual-sided vendor arbitrage printed ARR while manufacturing the discoverable artifacts that destroyed its exit multiple. The game ends with a new contest opened and unscored: pricing enforcement on non-coalition books, against which the winning side's instruments are mistargeted.
