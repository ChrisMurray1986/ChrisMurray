# Block 4: out-of-frame seeds x D2 x W3

## B4G1 — Federal Preemption Shock (D2 × W3, brief A)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** Employer-Refereed Private Ordering

By end-2030 the sector has settled into a privately refereed, contractually channeled attrition equilibrium. No public regulator meaningfully governs automated utilization review: state law is preempted for self-funded volume, judicially split for fully-insured volume, CMS is attestation-only, and EBSA has produced only persuasive letter language. Yet the conflict is not lawless. A dense layer of bilateral fee-computation and process paper - clawbacks, reversal-netting, pre-pay parity, SLA riders with interest, gold-card criteria, documentation exchanges, machine-readable audit feeds - now performs the refereeing function, drafted mostly by employer-fiduciaries and enforced through RFP cycles, independent audits, and discoverability. The automation contest has stabilized along data-moat lines: providers own the clinical-record/overturn surface, the national payer owns the billing-edit surface, and both keep escalating inside those boundaries at elevated but priced intensity. Disputed volume is being slowly drained at the edges by direct contracts and dispute-prevention instruments. A large deferred litigation bill (breach exhibits, weaponized patient bots, 20-25 percent shock ledger) hangs over the payer side with landings due 2031-2035, outside the game window. Nothing was resolved; everything was priced, papered, and relocated.

**Summary:** Game B4G1 (doctrine D2, world W3, preemption seed) opened with the scripted shock: federal preemption of state AI-UR statutes and attestation-only 0057-F enforcement. W3 physics promptly fractured the shock itself - a district-court carve-out for fully-insured books, a circuit split, cert denied - freezing two legal universes through 2030, with only the ERISA/self-funded book certain. Both escalation stacks concentrated there, and the game became a contest over who would referee that book once the state left the field. The answer was the employer. Green's Model Addendum, intercepted by and then merged with Red's Glass Box paper at the January 2028 jumbo signings, installed clawback and reversal-netting economics that made NationalCo's payment-integrity yield a function of denial accuracy rather than volume. Red never de-escalated from cost pain; it re-tiered, relocating extraction upstream to pre-pay itemized-bill/DRG review where appeal engines never fire, and when Green's shadow audit forced the v2.1 pre-pay parity amendment, Red converted accuracy-warranted fee refunds into its retention product. The contest never cooled; it migrated, and each migration was captured by another fee-computation instrument. Blue refused symmetric escalation, industrialized the never-appealed 40% behind the clinical-record data moat, cashed Lakeview's dossier at the one renewal moment friction favored providers, and used DTX benchmarks plus the Blue-Green SPV to arm fiduciaries and shrink the disputed denominator. Courts and agencies functioned purely as pricing: the single live ERISA fees-on-churn suit settled into a public template, EBSA closed with prudent-practice letter language that every carrier's counsel now quotes, and Red's litigation-shock ledger ended at 20-25 percent with all landings projected 2031-2035, post-game. The mandatory 2029 UM-analytics breach cratered health-AI trust on every side, rebuilt patient appeal-bots as auto-filing litigation machines, and repriced the Vendor's exit at a conflict discount. Final ordinal standing: Green first on all three of its metrics; Red second, holding every scored condition at visible margin cost; Blue third, missing only the moderate leakage decline; Vendor fourth, exiting real but well below its 2027 thesis. The decisive lesson of the excursion: removing the public referee did not produce chaos or detente - it produced private ordering, with the plan sponsor's contract, not the state, as the binding rail.

## B4G2 — Vertical Integration Wave (D2 × W3, brief B)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** Zoned Armistice with a Migrating Front

By end-2030 the conflict has not ended; it has been zoned. Each actor holds a fenced sanctuary it can defend and has stopped contesting the rest. Red (NationalCo) retains uncapped contingency payment-integrity on pre-pay/pre-claim surfaces across the vast non-coalition ASO market — a surface where clawbacks mathematically cannot fire because claims are corrected before payment, and whose legal status two contradictory CMS FAQs left open. Green's coalition book is governed end-to-end by its own procurement architecture: fee clawbacks, edit-level transparency, vertical-conflict audit rights, and pre-pay accuracy parity at anchor accounts, with the template escaping to roughly four non-coalition jumbo employers. Blue holds the clinical-appeals surface it leapfrogged (which Red vacated), an early-adopter shared-infrastructure cooperative (Corridor, FTC-cleared, six external systems), and a niche but growing employer direct-contract channel; its leakage, adverse-action costs, and margins all improved through the final turn, at the price of a live qui tam whose discovery runs past the horizon. The Vendor duopoly no longer exists: structurally separated, half-sold at a conflict-discounted multiple, its surviving half pivoting to appeal-provenance verification. Every constraint that actually binds is private ordering — bilateral process terms, fee schedules, procurement standards, performance guarantees — while regulators whipsawed incoherently and the state statute produced symbolic compliance only. Escalation is moderate and declining, but this is an armistice of exhaustion-of-reachable-targets, not peace: the war has migrated upstream to a surface where the other side's paper does not yet reach, and both sides are already armed for the rulemaking or circuit ruling that will decide it after 2030. Vertical integration, the seed threat, was blunted into a single acquisition and a standstill by countervailing employer structures, media cost, and vendor price-inflation.

**Summary:** B4G2 (D2 escalation-viable doctrine, W3 hostile/incoherent regulator, vertical-integration seed, GREEN and VENDOR active) resolved not as a war won but as a board progressively fenced. Turn 1 was asymmetric: Red ran three books on three physics — maximum payment-integrity intensity on ASO fee revenue, moderation on fully-insured, Stars-protective optics on MA — while executing the board-approved provider-acquisition program; Blue deliberately conceded contested surfaces to build Corridor, dual-track the Summit approach, and lock process terms at Lakeview. The seed event fizzled productively: Summit extracted price discovery and a Green direct-contract lifeline instead of selling, and a leaked services-arm deck raised the acquisition program's political price permanently. Turn 2 was the repricing: NationalCo preemptively conceded the fee clawback on overturned denials before Green's split award could fire, settled the ERISA action, and executed the game's decisive maneuver — a mix shift to clawback-proof pre-pay contingency while gold-carding and steering shrank the denial arena Blue had just leapfrogged onto. The W3 trust shock arrived early, halving appeal-bot volume and vindicating every actor built on adjudicated outcomes rather than bot-fattened statistics. Turn 3 partitioned the board: Lakeview's renewal merged both sides' drafts nearly clause-for-clause; Green bound pre-pay clawback parity at anchor accounts but could not reach the non-coalition book; Corridor cleared the FTC CID and its external-adopter threshold; Red harvested Stars, paused acquisitions, and kept its uncapped pre-pay sanctuary; the Vendor sold the war in pieces at a multiple discounted by exactly the conflict record Green spent three turns documenting. Three dynamics governed everything. First, constrained recovery channels displaced rather than deflated — every binding constraint on Red produced migration to an unconstrained surface within a turn, ending at pre-pay edits whose legal status CMS left contradictory. Second, the employer channel, not any regulator, was the only constraint that ever bound Red's self-funded payment-integrity behavior; contract-language contagion through jumbo RFPs outran statute by years while the state AI-UR law produced audit letters and zero fines. Third, W3 taxed each turn's front-runner through media, discovery, and procurement rather than enforcement — and defensible published records (attestations, compliance charters, adjudicated-outcomes methodology) proved to be armor, not exposure. The vertical-integration seed itself underdelivered: one distressed system acquired at a pincer-inflated price, Summit escaped, standstill by Turn 3. The game ends with escalation moderate, all binding architecture privately ordered, and the successor conflict — whether a pre-payment edit is an adverse benefit determination — explicitly unresolved.
