# Block 1: D2 escalation-viable x W2 slow world

## B1G1 — Arms Race Unbound (D2 × W2, brief A)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** Purchaser-Metered Attrition

By 2030 the arms race has not ended - it has been metered. Both machine arsenals remain fully deployed and are still probing new surfaces (Red expanding prepay validation into professional and outpatient surgical claims; Blue's first-pass engine chasing it a year behind), but the contest has settled into a purchaser-audited, contract-governed attrition equilibrium. The load-bearing architecture is entirely private: machine-readable criteria delivery, annual criteria publication, gold-card schedules with deemed-approval SLAs, employer-directed data pass-throughs to conflict-free auditors, fee true-ups tracing recoveries to overturns, and trend guarantees with fees at risk. No statute, court ruling, or CMS action binds anything by the horizon - three enacted state AI-UM laws are pre-operational, the anchor fiduciary suit settled cash-only and confidential, and the AG file never lit. The payer side's economics survived but changed quality: contingency arbitrage on captive accounts gave way to flat-fee-plus-guarantee at every organized account, meaning the payment-integrity product is now underwritten rather than extracted wherever purchasers organized. The provider side stopped losing and bent leakage late via prevention-at-source and industrialized appeals, but remains structurally one rotation cycle behind the payer's logic changes. The purchaser coalition is the de facto regulator of the system - its audit, clawback, and comparator instruments are the only constraints that ever bound - while the unorganized employer majority remains locked on the payer's paper, so the equilibrium is two-tier: disciplined competition at organized accounts, continued extraction at unorganized ones. The intermediary arms-dealer model was structurally liquidated. A large deferred-liability tail (litigation discovery, statute operationalization, an admission-shaped auto-reversal dataset) is scheduled to land in 2032-36, entirely outside the game window - several actors explicitly priced their endgame around outrunning it.

**Summary:** Game B1G1 opened with maximum-capability postures on all sides under escalation-viable doctrine (D2) in a slow-moving environment (W2), and the environment's inertia proved decisive: regulators, courts, and CMS never bound anything inside the horizon, so every constraint that actually bit was private. Turn 1 saw Red bank durable yield on ASO post-pay payment integrity (CD gap ~5, fee revenue with no MLR drag) while Blue paid formation costs on appeals automation, pooled payer-behavior data, and prevention builds. The strategic surprise was Green's organizing speed: the purchaser coalition formed inside the window Vendor assumed was open, and its ownership-mapping exhibit made the duopoly's both-sides structure a permanent discoverable fact. Turn 2 was the pivot: Green's clawback riders made post-pay recovery fee-toxic, so Red migrated the integrity engine upstream to prepay validation, where a dollar never paid generates no overturned recovery - a textbook constrained-channel migration. Blue reached production on the appeals utility and began strip-mining the never-appealed 40% of denials, arresting but not reversing leakage. Turn 3 sorted the board: Blue compiled Red's contractually published prepay criteria into a first-pass submission engine (making claims pass the denial machine rather than fighting it); Lakeview converted its accumulated abrasion record into the best paper Blue signed all game; GovProgramCo executed a cold, well-timed taper of its Stars-sunset MA intensity step-up just ahead of the 2032-36 shock landing window; and NationalCo converted contingency arbitrage into flat-fee-plus-trend-guarantee at every organized account under employer pressure. Green finished first on own-book economics: clawbacks collected, prepay fees trued up against verified outcomes, a live split-award comparator pricing Red's retention, and the one-page data pass-through rider becoming the game's most imitable diffusion vector. Vendor's both-sides thesis died in Green's exhibit; it demerged mid-inquiry and half-exited below its founding multiple. The signability gate mattered throughout: zero numeric denial-rate corridors were ever signed or even seriously proposed; every binding instrument was process, data-access, audit, gold-card, or fee-verification paper. Escalation ran high for two turns and eased to elevated by the scoring turn - not because either combatant exhausted, but because the purchaser channel repriced the fight and automation made selective concessions costless. Final ordering: Green first, Red and Blue nearly level (Red richer cumulatively, Blue steeper at the close), Vendor solvent, separated, half-exited, with the shock accumulator unfired and hanging over 2032-36.

## B1G2 — Detente Corridor (D2 × W2, brief B)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** The Price-List Armistice (purchaser-audited migration equilibrium)

By end-2030 the conflict has neither burned out nor been settled by treaty; it has been priced. The adversarial contest over the disputed healthcare dollar migrated twice — first out of prior authorization (retired publicly, on schedule, as cover) into pre-pay claim review on self-funded volume, where payer capability advantages were largest and nothing was visible to members, statistics, or regulators; then, in the final eighteen months, that invisible surface itself was dragged under contract: disclosed at category level, fee-capped, audited by conflict-certified independents, subject to reconsideration SLAs, and reconciled against plan assets. The mechanism that accomplished this was not government — the slow-world regulator spent five years issuing attestation pathways, unfunded statutes, and one single-digit fine — but the purchaser: employer fiduciaries converted CAA duty into procurement leverage, demonstrated a credible defection channel exactly once, and thereafter sold retention to carriers at the price of transparency. The end-state is a lattice of bilateral, renewal-priced process instruments (edit-library disclosure, change notice, gold-card and clean-claim certification exhibits, fee reconciliation, carve-out rights) laid over a capability contest that continues at reduced amplitude: the payer capability lead narrowed from decisive to marginal on coalition volume but persists, unshielded providers outside the coalitions still pay full freight, and utilization-management intensity has rebalanced onto Medicare Advantage surfaces that the 2029 ratings overhaul stopped scoring. The both-sides vendor that tolled the conflict was forced by procurement paper — not regulation — to dismember itself. Nothing converged to a market norm: every binding instrument is signatory-level, external diffusion reached only early adopters, and the one live lawsuit will produce its first public fee record after the horizon. It is an equilibrium of documented, audited, contractually-metered adversarialism — cheaper per disputed dollar, vastly better recorded, and stable only until the 2031 renewal wave reprices the carve-outs that survived.

**Summary:** GAME B1G2 (D2 escalation-viable doctrine, W2 slow world, GREEN and VENDOR cells active) opened on a negotiated Blue posture and ended, per the closing adjudication, "not with a treaty but with a price list." The defining dynamic was surface migration under visibility arbitrage: NationalCo's Turn 1 prior-auth retreat was executed as promised and simultaneously hollowed out, with its frontier stack redeployed to pre-pay itemized-bill/DRG review on ASO volume — a 3-4 point capability differential on a surface producing no appealable denials and no member-visible events. Under D2 physics that captured the majority of contested swing for two turns. Blue declined symmetric escalation, holding the Lakeview-RegionalMutual process accord (the game's durable detente kernel, extended to pre-pay surfaces by 2030 for a 2032 lock) while building a trustee-administered data pool that cut the capability gap to 1-2 points by production in mid-2029 — late enough that Blue missed its leakage target and finished third. The decisive force was Green. The employer cell converted CAA fiduciary duty into procurement leverage, and one anchor-account defection at the 2028 awards repriced NationalCo's entire posture: fee caps, claim-line feeds, auditor panels, then true-ups and flat-fee elections once the independent auditor's reconciled pre-pay numbers landed in 2029. Red conceded transparency repeatedly but never from cost pain — its self-funded PI engine stayed fee-positive throughout, and every concession was proximately triggered by employer abrasion, auditor documentation, or litigation pendency, always exchanged for term locks. GovProgramCo executed the quiet trade of the game, migrating UM intensity onto MA surfaces the 2029 Stars overhaul stopped scoring. The Vendor duopoly had the best opening turn (arming both sides, tolling the conflict) and the worst endgame: Green's conflict-certification standard progressively documented its both-sides economics into procurement files, a national feature story, and a surviving ERISA pleading, compressing its exit multiple until Project Cleave — self-dismemberment under procurement pressure — became the rational close. The regulator mattered almost entirely through inaction: attestation-based 0057-F compliance, unfunded SB1120 statutes, a sealed Lokken settlement, and a deregulatory Stars rulemaking that enabled rather than constrained. Final ordinal: Green, Red, Blue, Vendor. Every binding instrument in the game was a process or pricing term; no numeric denial-rate corridor was ever proposed, let alone signed; and no instrument diffused beyond early external adopters by the bell. Escalation ended moderate and contractually metered, not extinguished.

## B1G3 — Regulatory Theater (D2 × W2, brief A)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** Quiet-Front Attrition: a member-invisible, contract-governed war of profitable escalation

By end-2030 the conflict has neither resolved nor exploded; it has professionalized and gone underground. Adversarial payment-integrity activity continues at elevated intensity, but it has been deliberately rotated onto surfaces that no enforcement mechanism, regulator, scoring metric, or patient tool can see: low-overturn technical recoveries (COB, DRG validation, itemized-bill review, credit balances) on self-funded volume, booked as fee revenue. Member-facing denials and prior authorization are quiet - not from peace but from arbitrage, because those were the only surfaces anyone measured. The regulatory state is functionally absent: one thin statute produced attestation-grade compliance and a single small fine; CMS forbearance rolled forward; courts delivered one narrow settled count. In the state's absence, a dense private-ordering layer emerged and became the operative rulebook: bilateral process-term paper (algorithm disclosure, escalation SLAs with interest, criteria-based gold-carding, audit rights, capped fees, overturn clawbacks) diffusing at commercial speed through renewal riders, vendor repricing, and employer RFP exhibits - while every multilateral instrument (data trusts, attestation consortia, certification councils) stalled at its signatories. All principal combatants exited profitable: Blue on appeals recovery and collected contract paper, Red on rotated technical yield and stabilized retention, Green on invoiced (if partially starved) clawbacks and a defensible fiduciary file, the Vendor on a partial exit. Nothing is settled; the three-year locks, the unbuilt technical counter-corpus, and the deferred fee-economics renegotiation all point the same conflict into the 2031-32 renewal cycle with sharper tools on every side.

**Summary:** GAME B1G3 (D2 escalation-viable doctrine, W2 slow-referee world) opened with both coalitions committing their primary theater to law and regulation, and the world declined to show up. Of Blue's three target states, one enacted a statute stripped to terms Red already satisfied; CMS chose attestation-based forbearance three years running; courts moved on their own clocks, killing most of Green's exemplar suit on standing. The seeded legal-primary posture was devalued by the end of Turn 1, and every actor that pivoted fastest to private ordering and unit economics profited.

The real contest ran on two rails. First, a capability race: NationalCo's frontier-model post-pay push on the ASO book opened a CD gap of roughly 4; Blue's pooled Data Trust corpus clawed it back to roughly 2 by 2028; Red then rotated its matured, in-sourced stack into technical recovery domains (COB, DRG validation, itemized-bill, credit-balance) where Blue's clinical corpus offered no defense, re-opening a CD-3 band through scoring. Second, a paper race: Green weaponized award season to force fee caps and overturn clawbacks - and Red defused the confrontation by conceding preemptively, wrapping the concession in three-year locks that ran past final scoring. The decisive endgame insight was Red's: every enforcement mechanism on the board (clawbacks, appeal-bot sensors) keyed on overturns, so Red stopped producing overturns, escalating instead in single-digit-overturn technical domains that were simultaneously clawback-immune and member-invisible.

Numeric corridors never reached any table; process terms bound everywhere - disclosure, SLAs with interest, criteria-based gold-carding, audit rights, capped fees - through three separate commercial channels, including Red voluntarily selling as an ASO sales differentiator the very transparency substance it refused to have compelled on ASO lines. Blue's best money was its least glamorous: the never-resubmitted-40% appeals engine was ledger-positive from month one and scaled coalition-wide. The Vendor, Turn 1's only unambiguous winner, spent Turns 2-3 buying survivability from its own discoverable conflict record, split itself, and sold half.

At scoring, every actor's headline metrics were flat-to-improving while the escalation index stayed elevated - a profitable, professionalized, member-invisible war of attrition governed by bilateral contract rather than by the state, with the decisive battles (2031-32 renewal cycle, technical counter-corpus, gross-recovery clawback triggers, Lokken class certification) all deferred past the horizon.

## B1G4 — Asymmetric Squeeze (D2 × W2, brief B)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** The Purchaser's Armistice: priced coexistence through private ordering, with the contest rotated onto member-invisible surfaces

By end-2030 the market has settled into a purchaser-brokered, contract-mediated coexistence. The payer-provider AI conflict was not won, adjudicated, or regulated away — it was priced and partitioned by private paper. Employers, not regulators, functioned as the effective disciplinary authority: fiduciary purchasing standards, audits, clawbacks, and award conditioning converted the payer's payment-integrity fee engine from free revenue into fees-at-risk, and the two competing transparency standards merged in substance (the payer's charter absorbed the purchaser rider's economics under its own brand). The adversarial frontier did not close; it rotated. Denials and member-visible friction fell continuously — front-end prior auth was traded away early as cheap peace — while recovery activity migrated stepwise to member-invisible, low-reversal arithmetic surfaces (post-pay DRG, then itemized-bill/COB/repricing) that sit beyond both provider documentation defenses and clawback mechanics. Providers survived by two asymmetric routes: capability defense anchored in documentation origination (the one data terrain payers cannot buy), and outright dispute removal via prospective-payment direct contracts that took rural volume out of the adjudication economy entirely. No provider tier suffered a distress event at the table, though licensed diffusion of the payer's adjudication platform to regional carriers seeds a repeat of the squeeze one tier down after 2030. Every signed instrument is a process term — disclosure, notice, audit rights, clawbacks, attachment finality, gold-card criteria; no numeric denial-rate corridor was ever even proposed. The vendor duopoly's arsonist-and-fire-brigade model proved self-limiting: discoverability of its conflicts, not litigation outcomes, forced structural separation. Litigation and statutes accumulated as post-2030 contingencies (Lokken parked on a term sheet, six clinician-determination statutes not yet biting, RADV-adjacent exposure on the Stars arbitrage) rather than in-game forces. It is a stable-but-rotating armistice: cooperative paper on visible surfaces, continued algorithmic contest on invisible ones, with the purchasing channel holding the only whip that demonstrably works.

**Summary:** GAME B1G4 ran an escalation-viable doctrine (D2) inside a slow-referee world (W2) with employer and vendor cells active, seeded with consolidated payers pressing automated adjudication onto an un-resourced small-provider tier. Turn 1 set the signature pattern: Red harvested the weakest counterparties with a frontier pre-pay engine on member-invisible ASO surfaces while conspicuously de-escalating everything regulators, Stars, and employers could see. Blue's opening thesis — industrialize cheap appeals against an 80%-overturn denial pool — died at birth because Red deliberately shrank that pool and migrated contested dollars into DRG and itemized-bill documentation fights. From Turn 2 the game's real currency was signed paper. Green's rider-or-walk award season produced the structural event of the game: RegionalMutual signed the Fiduciary Data & Fee Rider on straightforward defection math, giving one standard a carrier's ink; the purchaser-selected audit documented contingency fees retained on recoveries later reversed, arming clawback and fiduciary leverage simultaneously. Blue stopped fighting where the capability gap was 3+ and redeployed onto the one terrain Red could not buy — documentation origination — while removing disputes entirely through Prairie's prospective-payment wraparound. Turn 3 resolved by convergence rather than collision. NationalCo bought its horizon (Charter 3.0 locked to 2031-32) at Green's price: cash restitution, 100% prospective clawback parity, independent recovery definitions, purchaser-selected auditors. Red declined the DRG fight Blue had finally won and rotated its fee engine onto itemized-bill, COB, and repricing arithmetic — surfaces packet layers cannot read and where reversal rates are too low for clawbacks to bite. GovProgramCo executed a two-turn Stars calendar arbitrage, re-tightening the year complaint and appeal measures exited scoring. Vendor bisected itself to salvage exit value from a conflict ledger that had become diligence poison. The formal environment never bound: no statute bit in-window, courts produced only cash settlements, CMS enforced by attestation, and the DOL file closed without action. Every constraint that actually changed payer behavior originated in the employer purchasing channel or bilateral contract. Escalation ran elevated, elevated, then moderate — declining in the scoring turn of an escalation-viable game because the disputed pool itself shrank. Final accounting: Blue's leakage bent moderately down on defended and removed volume; Red's yield, retention, and Stars position held or improved; Green converted paper into realized cash; Vendor exited half-sold. Nearly every actor finished above the drift line — the game's quiet verdict is that under a slow regulator, private ordering priced the conflict rather than any side winning it.
