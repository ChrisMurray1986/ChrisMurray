# Block 2: D1 baseline x W2 slow world (doctrine control)

## B2G1 — Arms Race Unbound (D1 × W2, brief B)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** The Priced Armistice

By end-2030 the system settled into a dyad-level contractual armistice inside a still-armed market. The principal payer-provider pairs bound bilateral process instruments - reconciliation queues with clinician-to-clinician SLAs, structured evidence at submission, machine-readable adverse-action rationale, gold-card charters with notice-and-cure stability terms - that deflated both sides' admin lines by shrinking the low-dollar, negative-yield dispute pool while leaving high-dollar pre-pay review fully armed. Payment-integrity economics were structurally repriced rather than abolished: the true-up/clawback mechanism converted vendor and carrier PI fees from earned-on-action to earned-on-outcome-sustained, and that accounting change - not any statute - is the game's most consequential export. The employer coalition ended with a credible priced exit: a minority-share direct-contract channel, paradoxically legitimized by the incumbent carrier administering its rails via wraparound. Regulation was inert throughout and remains so at the horizon: API mandates in serial forbearance until 2031, litigation producing sealed cash and no precedent, state clinician-determination statutes years from bite. Beneath the armistice, one combatant is quietly re-escalating - MA utilization management intensifying behind discovery-proof compliance paper the moment the Stars measures stopped pricing it. The vendor duopoly, whose revenue model was the war itself, ended structurally separated, commercially diminished, and exiting at a discount. Nothing diffused beyond the table: every instrument that worked is confined to signatories and a handful of external adopters, with market-wide spread sitting on 10-15 year curves. The conflict was repriced, instrumented, and contained - not resolved.

**Summary:** GAME B2G1 (D1 x W2) opened as a pure arms race and closed as a priced armistice. The defining Turn-1 move was Red's dual track: a public prior-auth retreat running alongside migration of the real economic engine into pre-pay edits, downcodes, and clinical-validation flags - a venue no denial-rate statistic measures - winning the timing race against Blue's 12-24 month build clocks. The counterweight formed the same turn: Green organized a full turn faster than Red priced, tolled its anchor renewals, and engineered the 2028 renewal collision. That collision resolved the way employer markets resolve: no carrier signed Green's standard unamended, but NationalCo forked it - conceding audit rights, data feeds, and fee election while amputating the cap and broad clawback. The standard died; its substance became the fastest-diffusing transparency paper in the game, carried by Red. Green then executed the pre-committed anchor defection, proving the walk-away was real. Blue's asymmetric bet matured on schedule: Meridian's pivot from starved CMS-0057-F feeds to owned 835 remittance data reached production in 2028 and moved pre-submission in 2029-30, converting the war from rework to prevention. Turn 3's hinge was an uncoordinated convergence - Lakeview and RegionalMutual independently drafted halves of the same pre-pay reconciliation instrument, which White consolidated: asymmetric, category-level de-escalation where the grind had gone negative-yield for both, with full pre-pay review retained on high-dollar lines. Both admin lines deflated for the first time since 2026; the Vendor, which had billed the trench war, paid for the peace. Crucible's two documented COI refusals compounded into procurement exile; its structural-separation cure ripened one quarter too late to matter commercially, and its exit launched at a litigation-overhang discount. GovProgramCo ran the coldest play on the board: MA UM intensification timed to the day the Stars complaints/appeals measures sunset, wrapped in discovery-proof no-targets paper that drew favorable regulatory contrast instead of cost. The environment stayed molasses throughout - serial 0057-F forbearance, class certification denied, sealed cash settlements, symbolic state fines, patient appeal-AI plateauing near 10%. Regulation never bound anything; the employer renewal cycle was the game's only functioning court. The war ended not in victory but in repricing: PI fees converted from earned-on-action to earned-on-outcome, disputes shrunk where mutually negative-yield, and the conflict was contained by bilateral paper among nine entities rather than resolved by any standard, statute, or judgment.

## B2G2 — Detente Corridor (D1 × W2, brief A)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** Buyer-Enforced Process Detente (The Invoiced Peace)

By end-2030 the payer-provider claims war in this excursion has not been won, lost, or regulated away — it has been contractualized. The operative order is a lattice of bilateral, renewal-gated process instruments: machine-readable edit-library disclosure, 75-day change notice, mutual audit protocols with cure periods and interest, published gold-card entry/exit criteria extended from prior authorization into claims review, pre-pay proportionality and audit-in-lieu terms, and fee structures converted from contingency-on-recovery to warranted flat fees with overturn-linked rebates. No party ever signed, or even proposed, a numeric denial-rate commitment; the peace is made entirely of procedure and pricing, never of outcomes. The enforcement mechanism is not government — CMS stayed attestation-and-forbearance throughout, courts settled everything confidential and cash-only with no precedent, and state statutes will not operationalize until years after the horizon. The enforcer is the employer-purchaser: ERISA fiduciary leverage, RFP awards, fee clawbacks, and audit rights made transparency the price of keeping the ASO book. Adverse-action activity migrated upstream (denials to pre-pay itemized-bill/DRG review) when the denial channel was contested, then shrank on disclosed books as evidence-complete submission and proportionality terms sterilized the new battleground. Critically, the peace is partial and bordered: it covers the table's signatory books and one payer's network via a standard renewal rider, while the non-signatory long tail continues to experience unfenced denial drift (~0.2pp/yr) with sagging overturn rates — a widening disclosed-versus-opaque divide that is the most likely ignition source for the next escalation cycle. The conflict-services vendor layer was forced to demerge and exit rather than toll the peace; its one durable invention, warranted-fee economics, was absorbed as everyone's standard. Nothing external rescued anyone: every maturity was purchased at the table, on contract clocks.

**Summary:** Game B2G2 ran doctrine D1 (negotiated posture) in world W2 (slow, forbearing environment) and produced a negotiated administrative peace enforced by purchasers rather than regulators. Turn 1 was a positioning turn: the one real event was the merger of Blue's and RegionalMutual's convergent transparency compacts into a single signed bilateral, priced with 2027 renewal commitments, while NationalCo scaled ASO payment integrity as a fee-revenue engine, Green put model terms with a PI-fee clawback into the RFP cycle, and the Vendor booked ARR while leaving conflict-of-interest paper on the ledger. Turn 2 was the hinge: RegionalMutual broke ranks and accepted Green's clawback terms to take Tranche A of the coalition book — a defection that was simultaneously Red's best retention defense and the event that converted Green's paper into the market ask. Meridian's two-year appeals/pre-adjudication build reached production and attacked the historically unfought 40% of denials; NationalCo rationally migrated recovery volume upstream into pre-pay itemized-bill and DRG review, producing the classic symmetric-escalation result on that axis — canceling headlines, inflating both admin ledgers. Green's fiduciary audits confirmed contingency fees booked on later-reversed recoveries and opened refund demands through NationalCo's own fee-credit rider. Turn 3 closed the loop: NationalCo capitulated at terminal-extension expiry (accepting the employer terms and the twice-refused edit-transparency annex) after opacity became a cost center; RegionalMutual extended Compact terms network-wide as its standard renewal rider — the only diffusion beyond the table; Annex B and evidence-complete claims sterilized the pre-pay battleground on disclosed books; GovProgramCo's calibration protocol defused its IRE-overturn exposure and disarmed Summit's dossier; and the Vendor, compounding COI discounts each turn, executed a real breakup and a discounted exit. Final ordinal ranks: Green first, Blue second, Red third, Vendor fourth. Analytically, three mechanisms did the work. First, the employer channel was the game's only binding constraint on payer behavior and only diffusion accelerant — regulation stayed symbolic, courts settled without process terms, statutes lagged past the horizon. Second, every instrument that bound was a process or fee term; numeric denial-rate commitments were never proposed by anyone. Third, everything ran on contract and deployment clocks: 12-24 month builds, 1-3 year renewal gates, diffusion reaching only 'early external adopters' by 2030. The peace is real but bordered — non-signatory books drifted worse all game, and that disclosed-versus-opaque gap is the residual instability the end-state carries forward.

## B2G3 — Regulatory Theater (D1 × W2, brief B)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** The Purchaser's Peace (governed core, rotating frontier)

By 2030 the system settles into a privately ordered, contract-mediated equilibrium brokered by purchasers rather than regulators. The public regulatory apparatus is decorative: statutes exist but are unenforced (one symbolic single-digit fine), no algorithmic-denial merits precedent exists, preemption is unresolved for want of ripeness, and CMS holds an attestation posture through the horizon. Real governance lives in bilateral paper: two full payer-provider transparency/audit rider dyads, three binding pre-claim certainty compacts, and a coalition-wide fiduciary fee rider (v3) that caps and claws back payment-integrity fees on roughly 2M employer lives. Inside the coalition perimeter, disputed-dollar volume, admin cost, and PI fee extraction all measurably fell; escalation ended low. Outside the perimeter, the market runs on the incumbent carrier's diluted variant — disclosure without fee caps — and the adverse-action contest has not ended but rotated into channels no table instrument measures: COB, subrogation, FWA, accuracy-as-a-service, and MA utilization management recalibrated to the post-2029 Stars measure set. It is a two-tier peace: a governed core surrounded by an unmeasured frontier, with the measurement apparatus (Blue's registry, Green's audit tests) perpetually one channel behind the payer's rotation. Latent legal exposure (GovProgramCo's dated memos, rising patient-AI appeal volume on recalibrated MA categories) persists past the horizon without crystallizing. The AI arms race itself became economically secondary: the vendor arms dealer exited repriced, and the decisive weapons were procurement conditions, discovery deadlines, and renewal calendars, not models.

**Summary:** Game B2G3 ran doctrine D1 in world W2 with GREEN and VENDOR cells active, seeded with legal/regulatory strategy as both coalitions' primary theater. The seed's premise self-liquidated within Turn 1: the W2 environment absorbed the entire statutory contest — bills passed without enforcement appropriations, Red's safe-harbor standard was converted to a voluntary SDO with no legal effect anywhere, the AG inquiry closed without action, and every consequential court event either settled sealed (Lokken), died on ripeness (Red's ERISA preemption suit), or delivered only procedural rulings. Across three turns, not one statute, precedent, or regulatory enforcement action ever bound any player's economics. "Regulatory Theater" proved literal: the theater consumed lobbying invoices and produced nothing adjudicable for either coalition.

The game's actual causal engine was purchaser power plus renewal-table contracting. Red executed the signature move in T1 — publicly retiring prior authorization while expanding pre-pay itemized-bill review and DRG validation on ASO volume — a substitution Blue's denial-keyed dossier missed for a full turn. Green, not Blue and not any regulator, imposed the first binding constraint on that fee engine: RFP awards conditioned on the Fiduciary Rider (two carriers executed at the T2 threshold), an anchor-jumbo defection, and a 408(b)(2) suit that survived standing and forced a pre-production settlement merging restitution, rider v3, and a discovery-benchmarked PEPM re-basing. Red conceded transparency and fee structure at every gate while never conceding the program itself, rotating yield into COB, subrogation, FWA, accuracy-as-a-service, and post-Stars-sunset MA channels that no instrument at the table measures.

The fastest-diffusing instrument was one the seed never contemplated: binding pre-claim adjudication certainty, pioneered by RegionalMutual's home-market peace and replicated at three dyads plus sponsor-facing riders — spreading because it made both signatories money. Cooperation, not escalation, moved every large number; the only structurally damaged cell was Vendor, whose dispute-pool-dependent book was repriced by the de-escalation it had quietly worked to prevent, forcing a split-sale exit at an infrastructure-lite multiple.

End-state: all three principal cells claim honest wins — Blue's moderate leakage decline, Red's held PMPM and frozen retention, Green's priced spend relief with a closed prudence file — while the underlying adverse-action contest continues in rotated, unmeasured channels. The coalition core operates on undiluted process-and-fee paper; the external market runs on Red's diluted conformed variant, diffused by incumbency and employer inertia, with settlement confidentiality having sealed the record that could have forced the undiluted version outward.

## B2G4 — Asymmetric Squeeze (D1 × W2, brief A)
**End-state (rapporteur-coined):** Papered Stalemate with Rotating Fronts

By end-2030 the payer-provider AI conflict has settled into a privately ordered, contract-administered cold peace in which neither side disarmed and neither side escalated. The fight persists at full automation on both sides but has been rerouted into channels the governing paper does not yet reach: the payer's recovery engine lives in prepayment edits, coding validation, COB/subrogation and itemized-bill mining rather than appealable clinical denials, while the provider coalition's defense lives upstream in pre-submission scrubbing and automated dispute filing. The binding governance layer is not statute or regulator — it is a lattice of bilateral fee, transparency, SLA, audit, clawback, and qualification-criteria terms, propagated through employer RFP cycles and renewal windows, enforced by joint operating committees, interest clocks, and independent arbiters. Regulation exists but is inert within the horizon (attestation-based CMS compliance, unfunded state rulemaking, one symbolic fine); litigation functions as a pricing mechanism for settlements, not a source of rulings. The employer coalition is the system's de facto regulator: its money moved first, its template won the standards fight, and the incumbent carrier's chief defensive innovation was to clone the coalition's terms inside its own wrapper to keep the lives. Structural features: the small tier survived through pooled shared-services infrastructure rather than purchased defense; the conflicted vendor was forced by counterparty screening — not by any regulator — to split its two-sided book and exit the conflict-arbitrage model; patient appeal-AI grew to ~8% of contested determinations as background pressure. The equilibrium is explicitly unstable at its edges: each new transparency instrument triggers a rotation of the recovery engine into the next unmeasured category, and the COB/subrogation frontier is the already-scheduled next war. Total friction cost has been automated down per unit but institutionalized as permanent infrastructure both sides now pay rent on.

**Summary:** B2G4 ran doctrine D1 (Blue refuses the symmetric automation race) in a W2 slow-regulator world with GREEN and VENDOR independently played, on a seed built for casualties: an undefended small tier, a capital-constrained Lakeview at 45% single-payer concentration, and a conflicted vendor selling defense to the victims. The casualties never materialized; the game instead produced three turns of accelerating contractualization. Turn 1 was positioning: Meridian built shared denial-defense infrastructure once for the coalition (Prairie paying the price of a year undefended), Red harvested the low-appeal tier behind a self-authored transparency shield, Green attached signable fee/process model terms to the live RFP wave, and Vendor's refusal to execute Blue's firewall addendum as drafted became the most consequential documented fact of the game. Turn 2 was the hinge: rather than test whether Green would really move jumbo lives, NationalCo folded the standards fight and merged Green's auditor-selection and overturn-clawback spine into its own rider — while simultaneously rotating its payment-integrity engine out of clawback-exposed clinical denials into prepay edits, coding validation, COB, and itemized-bill recoveries that sit outside the clawback by definition. The CAA suit survived MTD on fee-practice claims and priced a settlement rather than a ruling. Turn 3 closed with signing ceremonies, not rupture: Lakeview spent eight quarters of JOC scorecards at its renewal for category exits and an independent arbiter; Green extended reporting parity and a partial clawback into the categories Red had rotated into (COB/subrogation escaping beyond the horizon); NationalCo's Sponsor-Direct blocked Green's instrument by replicating its substance in-wrapper; Vendor executed a true ownership split and exited smaller, cleaner, at a compressed multiple. The regulator never mattered inside the window — CMS attestation regimes rolled forward, four largely Red-drafted AI-UR statutes produced one symbolic off-table fine, and the first binding constraint on payer behavior was employer purchasing power, every time. The adverse-action war did not end; it changed vocabulary. Denials became edits and recoupments, appeals became automated dispute filings, and both engines ran to a machine-speed stalemate with Vendor collecting rent from each side. Final ordinals: Green strongest (creditor with the completest file), Red intact but with declining yield quality on a defended book, Blue a partial win short of its moderate-leakage bar, Vendor middle — the conflict that built the business paid its discount on the way out. Escalation held at moderate for all three turns; every instrument tabled by Turn 3 cleared the signability gate, evidence the game taught all four cells to draft inside the whitelist.
