# Build-Out #8 — "From 94 to 25: The Operating-Model & Workforce Lab"
## Hands-on interactive segment for operational SVPs (30–45 min)

**Slot:** Segment 2 of the 2-hour block, immediately following the Revenue Cycle 2030 keynote — **the primary interactive segment for an operationally-led room** (the Arms Race War Game, Build-Out `01`, is the alternate for a CFO/payer-strategy-heavy audience).
**Duration:** 40 min core design, with a 30-min compression and 45-min extension noted throughout
**Audience:** ~5–8 tables of 6–8 operational Revenue Cycle SVPs and VPs (denials, AR, patient access, coding, CDI)
**Theme anchors:** Operating-model redesign (Theme 3), Workforce transition (Theme 4)

---

## 0. Design intent — why this, why these people

These are **operators at heart**. They run queues, staffing grids, productivity targets, and QA samples. They have almost certainly **not** yet sat down and redesigned their operating model or their workforce for an autonomous rev cycle — not because they can't, but because no one has handed them a concrete, grounded way in that isn't a vendor demo or a McKinsey deck. The failure mode with this room is **abstraction**: "future of work," "reimagine the org," "AI-enabled transformation." They will tune out in ninety seconds.

So this segment is built on three rules:

1. **Start in their shop, not in 2030.** Open on a denial analyst's actual Tuesday — the 43-item queue, 8–12 humans touching one denial — from the RCM 2030 POV. They will recognize it as their own operation, which earns the right to talk about changing it.
2. **Give them a worked example with real numbers, then make them do it on their own.** The POV already modeled a 94-FTE denials-and-AR pod down to 25, with a **named, defensible floor of 17**. That's not pie in the sky — it's an FTE bridge with rows you can point at. They replicate the method on their own function.
3. **Confront the hard operator questions out loud.** Who tells the biller? What do you say to your best coder who sees it coming? Which reqs do you stop backfilling *now*? How do you not lose the people you need *during* the transition? If we dodge these, it feels academic. If we put them at the center, it feels like Monday.

**Session promise (say it at the start):** *"For the next 40 minutes we're not going to talk about the future of work. We're going to redesign one pod you all run today, and figure out where its people go. Real workflow, real roles, real numbers. You'll leave with two things you can put in front of your team and your HR partner next week."*

---

## 1. The grounding — practical thought leadership (deliver as 4 short beats, not a lecture)

Weave these in as you go; do not front-load them. Each is a fact that makes the exercises feel real, not speculative.

**Beat A — "This is already happening, in your building, right now."**
> Ambient documentation and autonomous coding are *already* displacing transactional coding and documentation work — HFMA documents coders and documentation specialists shifting into exception management today, not in 2030. *"The transition isn't a decision you're going to make. It's a wave that's already moving through your coding department. The only decision is whether you shape it or get shaped by it."*

**Beat B — "The math is real, and someone already did it."**
> A modeled mid-size IDN denials-and-AR pod: **94 FTE → 25** in the automated state, **cost per denial $161 → $59**. But — and this is the part that makes it credible — there is a **floor of 17 FTE the function cannot cross**, set by human decision points (clinical-necessity attestation, high-dollar write-offs, rebill release). *"This isn't 'AI replaces everyone.' It's a bridge with named rows and a floor. You can argue with the number. You can't call it hand-waving."*

**Beat C — "94 to 25 is not a layoff plan. It's a reskilling plan — and most of the path is vacancies you already can't fill."**
> 43% of providers report being understaffed (Experian 2025); ~80% already outsource part of rev cycle. *"Run this at typical rev-cycle attrition and a large share of your reduction happens by not backfilling reqs you can't fill anyway. The question isn't 'who do I cut.' It's 'who do I redeploy, and what do I stop hiring for starting now.'"*

**Beat D — "The reskilling story IS your retention strategy."**
> AHA: staff increasingly stay for growth, meaningful work, and development — organizations slow to adapt lose talent to faster ones. *"Your best coder is going to read the same headlines you do. If you don't give her a path into exception work or AI oversight, a competitor will. The workforce transition isn't a threat to your retention — done right, it's the reason your best people stay."*

## 3. Cold open — "A Tuesday you'll recognize" (1 slide, from the POV)

Put Maria on the screen. Read it flat — let them recognize it.

> **Maria Alvarez — Denial Analyst — Tuesday, 9:47 AM.**
> 43 denials waiting in her Epic queue. Productivity target: 38 touches. No triage intelligence in front of her — *she is* the triage intelligence.
> By 3:20 PM: the denial that came back — same payer, same denial code, same edit as last month. Raised in the denial committee, action items captured. Nothing changed. No one owns prevention.
> 5:10 PM: 16 accounts touched, 3 closed. The rest wait on records, portals, payers, approvals. Typical cycle: 60–120 days. **8–12 humans touch one denial.**

**Facilitator:** *"Show of hands — whose operation does this describe? Keep them up. That's the room. Now here's the thing nobody tells Maria: within a few years, most of what she did today is done by an agent. The interesting question for you — the people who run Maria's world — is not whether that happens. It's what her pod looks like on the other side, and where Maria goes. That's what we're building for the next half hour."*

---

## 4. The worked example (1 slide — the credibility anchor)

Show the FTE bridge as a simple waterfall. This is the POV's model; present it as "here's one worked example, so you're not starting from a blank page."

| Step | Detail | FTE |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline** | Denials pod (39) + AR analytics pod (55) | **94** |
| − Eliminated | AR Reps 18 · Appeals Coord 6 · Cash-variance 2 · Sups 4 · Mgrs 2 | → 62 |
| − Transformed / productivity | Appeals Spec −5 · Denial Analyst −14 · AR Analyst −25 · Underpayment −2 | → 16 |
| + New roles | Pod Product Owner · Exception PM · Contract Intelligence · Continuous-Improvement Lead · Clinical Evidence Curator | → 23 |
| + Product-line allocation | | **25** |
| **The floor** | **17 FTE cannot be crossed** — set by human decision points: clinical-necessity attestation, rebill release, above-threshold write-offs, closed-loop learning owner, pod owner | — |

**Facilitator:** *"Two things I want you to take from this before you build your own. One: it's not zero — there's a floor, and the floor is made of judgment calls a machine doesn't get to make. Two: look at the bottom rows. Five roles that don't exist in your org chart today. The transition isn't just subtraction. It's a different-shaped team. Now let's build yours."*

## 5. EXERCISE 1 — Redesign the Pod (13 min, Theme 3)

**What each table gets:** a printed **current-state denial workflow placemat** (the POV's real map): Payer → Cash Poster posts 835 → Epic denial workqueue → Denial Analyst triages → branches to AR Rep/rebill, Appeals Coordinator, Underpayment, Mailroom/COB → Appeals Specialist + Physician Advisor → outcome in 60–120 days. Every box is a human touch. Plus a stack of **red "ELIMINATE," blue "AGENT," and green "HUMAN FLOOR" stickers** and three autonomy chips (T1 draft / T2 recommend-and-approve / T3 execute-and-audit).

**The three passes (facilitator calls them in sequence, ~4 min each):**

**Pass 1 — ELIMINATE ("Why does this step exist at all?").** *Before you automate anything, cross out the steps that shouldn't exist.* Red sticker on every box that a **prevention-at-source** move upstream (clean claim, documentation at the point of care, front-end eligibility/auth) would make unnecessary. *"The best denial workflow is the one that never starts. HFMA's line: don't ask how to do this faster, ask why this step exists at all. Kill before you automate — otherwise you're just automating dysfunction."*

**Pass 2 — AUTOMATE & ASSIGN AUTONOMY.** For every surviving box: blue "AGENT" sticker if an agent does the touch, and pick the autonomy chip — **T1** (agent drafts, human sends), **T2** (agent prepares, human approves, agent executes), or **T3** (agent executes routine, human sample-audits). *"You're not just saying 'automate it.' You're saying how much rope the agent gets, and who's watching. That's the governance conversation your board is going to ask you about — you're having it right now, on a real workflow."*

**Pass 3 — HUMAN FLOOR.** Green "HUMAN FLOOR" sticker on the boxes that **must** stay human and why — clinical-necessity judgment, high-dollar write-offs, attestation, the genuinely novel exception. *"This is the most important pass. If you can't defend why a human is on this step, an auditor and a CFO will both ask. And if you circle too few, you've built something you can't stand behind. The floor is your credibility."*

**Output (write on the placemat footer):** redesigned pod headcount delta (from __ to __), the **one step we were most surprised we could eliminate**, and the **one step we refuse to take a human off of**.

**Facilitator watch-fors:** tables that automate before eliminating (redirect them — Pass 1 first); tables that put zero on the human floor (push back — "you'd let an agent issue a charity write-off?"); tables that treat "AGENT + T3" as the answer for everything (ask what the exception rate is and who catches the miss).

## 6. EXERCISE 2 — Where Do Your People Go? (12 min, Theme 4)

This is the exercise the room has never done and most needs. Keep it concrete and humane.

**What each table gets:** a **role-evolution placemat** with today's roles down the left and three columns — ELIMINATED / TRANSFORMED (fewer, more senior) / NET-NEW — plus a starter map on the back they can accept or argue with:

| Today's role | Likely path | Into what |
|---|---|---|
| AR Rep (phone/portal follow-up) | Transformed / reduced | Exception Analyst — works only what the agent escalates |
| Denial Analyst | Transformed / reduced | Exception Analyst (senior) or Payer-Behavior Analyst |
| Coder | Transformed | AI Coding Auditor / Clinical Evidence Curator (reviews agent output) |
| Appeals Coordinator | Eliminated | (assembly is automated) |
| Appeals Specialist | Transformed / retained | Clinical judgment on necessity cases — reviews drafts, doesn't draft |
| Underpayment Analyst | Transformed | Contract Intelligence Analyst — governs machine-readable contracts |
| Supervisor / Manager | Transformed | Pod Product Owner · AI Operations Supervisor · Continuous-Improvement Lead |

**The three steps (facilitator calls them, ~4 min each):**

**Step 1 — Sort your roster.** Take a standard 20-person denials-and-AR pod (or your own numbers if you know them cold). Put each role in a column. *"Be honest. If you can't name the net-new role a person moves into, they're in the eliminated column — don't hide a hard call in the transformed column."*

**Step 2 — Do the redeployment math.** Of the people whose roles shrink or end: how many can you **honestly reskill** into the transformed and net-new roles? What's the skill gap — coding-to-AI-audit is short; AR-rep-to-contract-intelligence is long. What's the timeline? *"The POV says the scarce new roles take 18–24 months to build. That means your reskilling clock starts before the platform lands, not after. Who do you start developing now?"*

**Step 3 — Face the four questions (the grounding — do NOT skip).** On the placemat, one line each:
1. **Who do you tell, and when?** (The rumor is faster than your plan. Silence reads as a layoff coming.)
2. **What do you say to your best coder** — the one who's going to figure this out before you announce anything?
3. **Which reqs do you stop backfilling *now*** — so redeployment happens through attrition, not a reduction in force?
4. **How do you keep the people you need *through* the transition** — the ones whose institutional knowledge trains the agents and staffs the floor?

**Output:** a **90-day workforce starter** — the two reskilling paths you'll stand up first, the roles you'll stop backfilling this quarter, and the one sentence you'll say to your team before someone else says it for you.

**Facilitator watch-fors:** tables that put everyone in "transformed" (call the euphemism — force real eliminated calls); tables that skip Step 3 to stay comfortable in the boxes (that's the whole exercise — pull them back); the table that realizes it should freeze a backfill Monday (surface that in report-out).

## 7. Debrief provocations (facilitator script)

Q1 and Q2 mandatory; pick 1–2 more.

1. **The eliminate-first discipline (Theme 3):** *"How many tables automated a step in Pass 2 that they'd crossed out in Pass 1? None, because you did it in order. That order is the whole game. Every vendor will sell you Pass 2 — automate the step. Almost nobody makes you do Pass 1 first — should the step exist? The systems that win don't have faster denial workflows. They have fewer of them."*
2. **The floor is the strategy (Theme 3 → governance):** *"Look at what your tables put in green. That's not the leftover after automation — that's the actual job of your 2030 team. Judgment, exceptions, the calls a machine doesn't get to make. Your operating model isn't 'humans do less.' It's 'humans do only the things that need a human, and govern the machine that does the rest.' That's a smaller team doing harder work — which, by the way, is a better job than the one Maria has today."*
3. **The backfill decision is available Monday (Theme 4):** *"Some of you wrote 'stop backfilling AR-rep reqs.' That's the most important thing that happened at your table, and it costs nothing and needs no platform. Redeployment-not-layoff isn't a values statement — it's an operational discipline that starts with a hiring-freeze decision on specific roles this quarter. Who's actually going to do it?"*
4. **The best-coder question (Theme 4 → retention):** *"What did you decide to say to your best coder? Because here's the trap: the instinct is to say nothing until you have a plan. But your best people leave in the silence — they assume the worst and a recruiter offers them certainty. The counterintuitive move is to bring them in early as the ones who help build the exception function. You reskill your keepers by making them co-authors of the transition, not survivors of it."*
5. **The bridge to the keynote:** *"Remember Maria from the keynote, and Priya — the AI Operations Supervisor who runs 200 agents and never opens Epic Hyperspace? You just built the bridge between them. That's not a slide from a consultant. That's your placemat. The 2030 org chart isn't coming to you as a mandate — you just drew it."*

## 8. Close — the two Monday artifacts + the poll

**Artifact 1 — the redesigned pod placemat** (Exercise 1): they take it home and show their team what the work becomes.
**Artifact 2 — the 90-day workforce starter** (Exercise 2): they hand it to their HR business partner.

**The operator's first-90-days checklist** (leave-behind card — translated from the POV's call-to-action into operator language):
1. **Pick one pod** (denials & AR is the usual first) and draw its current-state map — the honest one, with every human touch.
2. **Run the three passes** on it — eliminate, automate-with-autonomy, human floor. Put a headcount and a floor on it.
3. **Freeze backfill on 1–2 role types** you can see shrinking, this quarter — start redeployment through attrition now.
4. **Name the two reskilling paths** you'll build first (coder→AI audit and denial-analyst→exception are the short ones) and pick the first three people.
5. **Declare your autonomy policy** — what agent independence you'll accept, on what work, with what human floor — before a vendor declares it for you.

**Closing poll (phones up):** *"What's the first thing you do when you're back?"*
- ☐ Draw the current-state map of one pod (honestly)
- ☐ Freeze backfill on a shrinking role type this quarter
- ☐ Name my human-decision floor before an auditor does
- ☐ Pick my first three reskilling candidates
- ☐ Have the honest conversation with my best coder

**Final line:** *"Not one of those needs a purchase order. Every one of them is an operator's decision — which is exactly why they're yours to make, and why no vendor is going to make them for you. You didn't just hear about the 2030 rev cycle today. You drew it, and you staffed it. Go run it."*

## 10. Risks & contingencies

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Feels abstract / "future of work" | The antidote is built in: real workflow, real roster, real numbers, Pass-1-before-Pass-2. If energy drifts abstract, pull back to the placemat: "cross out one box right now." |
| Room resists the headcount reality ("we're already understaffed") | Lean in — that's Beat C. "Exactly. That's why this is redeployment through vacancies you can't fill, not a cut. Show me a req you'd stop backfilling." |
| Someone hears "how to justify layoffs" | Front-load Beat C/D and the retention frame; Exercise 2 Step 3 is explicitly about keeping people. Say plainly: "this is a reskilling and retention exercise, not a RIF planner." |
| Tables stall on Exercise 1 Pass 1 | Give one example out loud: "the AR rep logging into four portals to check status — why does that step exist? Real-time API or a status agent kills it. Now find yours." |
| "Our coders/AR are unionized / civil service" | Legitimate and common — make it a design constraint, not a blocker: "then your reskilling paths and your redeployment timeline matter *more*, and your backfill-freeze is your main lever. Put it on the placemat." |
| Confidentiality on real numbers | The standard roster (20-person pod) lets them engage without disclosing; they can substitute their own privately. |
| Small room (<3 tables) | Run it as one group on a single large wall-mounted current-state map; facilitator holds the marker, room calls the passes. |

## 11. Connection points to the rest of the block

- **From the keynote:** the keynote already introduces Maria (today), Priya (AI Ops Supervisor, 2030), and Kai (payer network manager). End the keynote's operating-model section with *"…in a few minutes, you're going to build the bridge from Maria to Priya yourselves — on your own pod."* This segment *is* the keynote's operating-model act made hands-on.
- **To the leadership segment (03):** this segment ends on workforce and people decisions; Segment 3 opens with *"you just decided where your people go and what you'll say to them. Leading that conversation — and modeling the AI fluency you're about to ask of your team — is the hardest thing you'll do. Here's the data on why your personal practice is the culture strategy, and here's mine."* The handoff is natural: workforce transition → the leader who has to carry it.
- **Readiness Mirror threading (02):** the closing poll here is a Mirror station; the reserve question "What % of your rev cycle team could you honestly redeploy today?" (Mirror §3, R2) is the perfect opener for Exercise 2.
- **Relationship to the War Game (01):** keep the war game available as the alternate for a CFO/payer-strategy audience, or as a 10-minute compressed "and here's the payer dynamic your redesigned pod is operating inside" bridge if the room has both operators and finance chiefs. For a purely operational room, this Lab replaces it as Segment 2.
