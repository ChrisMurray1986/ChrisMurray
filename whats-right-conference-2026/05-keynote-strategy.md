# Build-Out #5 — Keynote Strategy: Leveraging the RCM 2030 POV
## How to turn a 5-section, analyst-grade POV into a 30-minute executive keynote that cannot be mistaken for a pitch

**Input analyzed:** `rcm_2030_pov_v6.html` — Huron POV, "The Future of Revenue Cycle in 2030"
**Constraints:** 30 minutes · SVP/CFO audience already saturated with AI pitches · failure mode = sounding like a vendor · success = they act differently Monday
**Companion docs:** run-of-show (`00`), war game (`01`), Mirror (`02`), leadership segment (`03`), purgatory autopsy (`04`)

# PART 2 — The 30-minute keynote architecture

**Organizing principle:** the POV's own build order is 2→3→4→1→5 (analyst logic). The keynote inverts to **money → people → futures → proof-of-safety → clock** (executive logic). Every beat opens with the room's pain (research stat), answers with the POV, and banks one quotable line.

### Beat 1 (min 0–4) — "A number you can actually underwrite"
- Entry state: Mirror Station 1 just ran; the 80%-adoption / 7%-prepared gap is on screen. Open: *"You've been pitched AI numbers all year. Here's what almost none of them survive: netting out what the agents cost to run."*
- Show the bridge ONE line at a time: $22M problem → five value drivers → **minus $1.5M agent OpEx** → $10.5M. Pause on the minus line: *"That's the line a CFO asks for first — and the line most decks hope you won't."*
- Name the conservatism: modeled ranges total $16–34M; the headline deliberately uses ~a third of midpoint. *"I'm showing you the number I'd defend to your board, not the number that wins a bake-off."*
- **Banked line:** "This is a $22M-a-year problem per billion of net revenue — and about half of it is recoverable at a price you can underwrite."

### Beat 2 (min 4–11) — "Three Tuesdays" (the persona arc)
- **Maria, today (2.5 min):** 43 in queue · 15 minutes of lookup per denial · 8–12 humans touch one denial · the 3:20 PM moment — *the same denial came back, committee met, nothing changed, no one owns prevention.* Land on the research echo: this is the no-learning-loop stall signature the whole industry shares. The room will recognize their own shop.
- **Priya, 2030-Automated (2.5 min):** 1,240 agent actions/hour, 91% straight-through · escalations arrive pre-packaged · anomaly→rule-change in hours · war room closed in 42 minutes · *"Priya never opened Epic Hyperspace."* Note explicitly: **Epic stays your EHR** — the platform around it changes (defuses the "we're an Epic shop" objection early).
- **Kai, 2030-Reimagined (2 min):** 14 overnight rule exchanges with six payers · one policy commit propagating in 40 minutes · a JOC with no denial report — *"No one on her team worked a denial today. The work isn't automated — it's eliminated."*
- **Banked line:** "Maria works denials. Priya supervises the agents that work denials. Kai lives in a world where the denial mostly doesn't exist. All three are the same budget line — the question is which one you're staffing for in 2030."

### Beat 3 (min 11–17) — The operating model (with the workforce sequence discipline)
- Open with THEIR pain, not the org chart: 43% understaffed, requisitions unfillable, a third of hours on hold music and portal logins. *"Nobody in this room can hire their way out — you've tried."*
- Then the bridge: 94 → 25 (→ ~13 in Reimagined), operators→stewards, the named NEW roles (AI Operations Supervisor, Contract Intelligence Analyst, Continuous Improvement Lead, Exception PM) — and the **17-FTE human floor** set by clinical attestation and judgment decision floors. The floor matters strategically: it tells the room this is engineered, not maximal.
- Same breath, verbatim from the POV: *"94→25 is not a layoff plan. It is a reskilling plan."* Back it with third-party cover: AHA documents upskilling + net-new roles as the sector pattern; HFMA documents coders already shifting to exception management; BCG's 10-20-70. And the practical note: at typical rev-cycle attrition, most of the reduction path is *absorbing vacancies you already can't fill*.
- **Optional insert point:** Pilot Purgatory Mode 1 (the five stall signatures, 10 min) extends this beat if the keynote gets 40 minutes. At 30, skip — the scorecard is in the packet.
- **Banked line:** "The org chart stops resembling a revenue cycle department and starts resembling a payer governance office."

### Beat 4 (min 17–22) — "What makes autonomy safe enough to scale" (governance as the trust answer)
- Bridge from the room's blocker: 41% can't fully trust AI results; 63% say auditability is mandatory. *"You're right on both counts. Here's what earning that trust mechanically looks like."*
- Show ONLY: the T0–T3 autonomy ladder (with promotion gates) + two controls chosen for CFO resonance — the **monthly AI-error cap** ("a pre-authorized dollar budget for agent mistakes; consume it and autonomy auto-drops a tier") and the **one-click pause**. Mention the other four exist; packet has all six.
- One 60-second command-center moment — the **timely-filing cliff drill-through** ($1.4M expiring in 6 days, invisible on a classic AR-days report, avoidance plays pre-sequenced on the same screen). It dramatizes leading-vs-lagging in cash terms, which is the only performance-management point a CFO needs from Section 4.
- **Banked line:** "Autonomy without governance is exposure. With a ladder and an error budget, Tier-3 agents become audit-defensible, reversible, and improvable — not a faith-based deployment."

### Beat 5 (min 22–27) — The clock (call to action, de-commercialized)
- The four clocks, all third-party facts: denials rising 5–8%/yr · scarce roles take 18–24 months to build · **CMS-0057-F prior-auth API mandate, January 2027** · learning-curve advantage compounds.
- First-90-days decisions, framed as *self-executable* (no Huron dependency): name ONE accountable owner (not a committee) · baseline your own cost-to-collect and write-off root causes · pick 2–3 lighthouse use cases · declare your autonomy policy — *"how much agent independence will your board accept, on what work? If you can't answer that, you don't have an AI strategy; you have a procurement backlog."*
- **Banked line:** "The 2030 revenue cycle gets built on this year's planning calendar — or it doesn't get built."

### Beat 6 (min 27–30) — The handoff (the two-futures question → war game)
- *"I showed you two 2030s. The Automated one you can build alone. The Reimagined one — where follow-up dissolves — you cannot, because the rule layer is shared with the people currently denying your claims. Whether that's naïve or inevitable isn't a technology question. It's a strategy question. So we're not going to debate it — we're going to play it. Welcome to 2028."* → Mirror Station 2 → war game cold open.

# PART 3 — The de-pitch protocol (specific edits and moves)

## 3.1 Pre-empt the number challenge — present the skeptic's case yourself
Before anyone can attack the $10.5M, attack it first, from the podium: *"Here's what you should be skeptical of: the 18% agent-OpEx benchmark is an industry figure, not a healthcare-specific one. The baseline assumes 94 FTE in denials and AR per $2B system — yours may differ. The Reimagined path needs payer cooperation that doesn't exist today. All fair. That's why the packet has the full assumptions table — swap in your numbers and re-rate the bridge on the flight home."* In a CFO room, prosecuting your own model is the highest-credibility move available, and it is the exact opposite of vendor behavior.

## 3.2 The give-away move (replaces the sales close)
Cut "Ready to price your baseline?" — nothing in the keynote may ask for a meeting. Replace with giving the model away: the leave-behind includes the **assumptions & inputs worksheet** (NPR, denial volume, initial denial rate, overturn rate, loaded salary, the OpEx range) exactly as the POV's "replace with your own numbers" footnote intends. The strategic logic: this room hires people who hand them the tool, not people who dangle it. Demand pulls; the restraint is the brand (same principle as `03` §9).

## 3.3 Third-party armor on every load-bearing claim
Every POV assertion that could sound self-serving gets a published external stat on the same slide, from the research report: no-learning-loop → the HFMA end-to-end-redesign quote (verified) · trust/auditability → Experian 41% + Black Book 63% · workforce → AHA/HFMA/BCG · urgency → CMS regulation. The POV already cites HFMA/MGMA/BLS salary benchmarks — surface those citations visibly. Huron's name appears on the title slide and nowhere else.

## 3.4 Objection prep (the five that will come)
1. **"We're an Epic shop; Epic will build this."** — POV's own answer, said early in Beat 2: Epic stays the system of record; the contest is the platform *around* it — orchestration, governance, contracts-as-code. What Epic ships, you'll still govern.
2. **"94→25 won't survive HR/board/union optics."** — Floor of 17 shows it's engineered; reduction path runs substantially through vacancies you can't fill and attrition you already have; the reskilling plan starts *now, in parallel* (POV language). The alternative — operators grinding portals while competitors redeploy — is the actual retention risk (AHA).
3. **"The Reimagined path assumes payers cooperate. Fantasy."** — Defer to the war game: *"Hold that thought for exactly twenty minutes — you're about to negotiate it yourselves."* (And CMS-0057-F makes the first API handshake mandatory, not optional.)
4. **"Where does the 18% OpEx figure come from?"** — Cross-industry benchmark for buy-mode agent deployments at mature state, range 12–25%; healthcare-specific data is young — which is why the model shows the range and the worksheet lets them re-rate.
5. **"Our AI spend is up and denials are up."** — Agree; that's the arms race (one system: +50% denials as payers adopted AI review) and pilot purgatory (53% stuck). *"Both of those have their own segments in the next ninety minutes."* The keynote doesn't have to carry every answer — the block does.

## 3.5 Language discipline
Keep the POV's mission-control vocabulary (**stewards, mission control, decision floors, error budget, "the work is eliminated, not automated"**) — it's distinctive and non-vendor. Ban from the spoken track: "solution," "offering," "capability," "our clients," "we can help you," any Huron service name. Say "the model," never "our model."

# PART 4 — Production notes

## 4.1 Slide inventory (≤14 slides)
1. Title (only Huron branding moment)
2. The bridge, built line-by-line, minus-OpEx line emphasized
3. The conservatism slide (modeled range vs. underwritten blend)
4–6. Maria / Priya / Kai — one slide each: photo-style header, three moments, one "so what"
7. The staffing dead-end (their pain: 43% understaffed + a third of hours on portals/holds)
8. The FTE bridge (simplified: 94 → 25 → ~13, new roles named, floor of 17 flagged)
9. "Not a layoff plan" + third-party cover (AHA/HFMA/BCG on one slide)
10. Autonomy ladder T0–T3
11. Error cap + one-click pause ("governance, operationalized")
12. Command center: timely-filing cliff drill-through (single frame)
13. The four clocks + first-90-days decisions
14. The two-futures question (handoff to war game)

## 4.2 Leave-behind additions (folds into the packet from `00`)
- The full POV (or its exec summary + assumptions worksheet) — the density cut from stage becomes the artifact's depth
- The first-90-days decision card with owner column (CEO/CFO/CHRO/CRO), formatted as a board-agenda insert
- The six-control governance framework one-pager (pairs with the Health Affairs six-risk checklist already in the packet — one names the risks, the other answers them)

## 4.3 Hard cut list (stage) — all preserved in leave-behind
Five KPI drill-throughs (keep only timely-filing) · layer-by-layer platform comparison · full org charts · 17-agent roster and clusters · FTE bridge row detail · NPV/perpetuity math · low/mid/high scenario bands (mention, don't show) · "How this was built" appendix.

## 4.4 Rehearsal checks
- Beat 2 is the overrun risk: cap each persona at its timestamp; the personas are moments, not walkthroughs.
- Dry-run the Beat 1 pause after the minus-$1.5M line — it's the keynote's credibility hinge; don't talk over it.
- Verify every third-party stat on slides against the research report's source register (three are adversarially verified; spot-check the rest — caveat noted in `research-report-session-themes.md`).
- Confirm CMS-0057-F status ~2 weeks before the session (a delay or final-rule change would be known to this room instantly).

## 4.5 Success test for the keynote
An attendee should leave Beat 6 able to repeat, without notes: **the honest number** ($10.5M net, and why "net" mattered) · **the three Tuesdays** (Maria/Priya/Kai) · **the floor** (17 humans, engineered not maximal) · **the error budget** (autonomy isn't faith-based) · **the two futures** (and that the second one runs through the payer relationship they're about to war-game) · **one 90-day decision with their name on it.** If they can also repeat a Huron service name, Beat 5 was done wrong.
