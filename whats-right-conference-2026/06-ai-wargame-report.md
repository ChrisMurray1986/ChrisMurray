# The Payer–Provider AI Arms Race: A Strategic Wargame Series, 2026–2030
## RAND-grade red team / blue team analysis — likelihood and impact of five potential futures

**Prepared for:** AI & Automation Revenue Cycle capability lead, Huron — supporting the "What's Right In Healthcare" executive session
**Method:** Two game series (replication design) × four excursions × three turns (2026–27, 2028, 2029–30); simultaneous-move Red (payer coalition) vs. Blue (provider coalition) with a neutral White Cell adjudicator bound to published base rates and institutional-friction rules; dual-lens futures panel (inside-view and outside-view judges per future); three-lens adversarial peer review (payer-realism, healthcare-inertia, wargame methodology); synthesis with refuted findings dropped and weakened findings restated in corrected form.
**How to read this:** Wargames of this type generate *mechanisms and hypotheses, not parameter estimates*. Every dollar band and probability in this report is directional unless tied to an external source. Where the two panel judges disagreed materially, the range is reported — not averaged into false precision. Limitations are disclosed in full in §8.

---

# 1. BOTTOM LINE

**Plan for regulation-bounded conflict, not a grand détente.** After adversarial review, the panel's averaged probabilities put negotiated détente (F2) at 28.5% and regulated equilibrium (F3) at 27% — statistically indistinguishable — with continued uncoordinated escalation (F1) at 18.5%, asymmetric squeeze (F5) at 16%, and structural bypass (F4) at 6.5%.

The headline conceals the study's most decision-relevant fact: **the inside (game-anchored) and outside (base-rate) judges diverged by 3–4x on the two biggest futures** — F2 scored 45% inside versus 12% outside; F1 scored 7% inside versus 30% outside. That divergence is not noise. Both game series ran under one adjudication doctrine that scores symmetric escalation as self-canceling and rewards cooperative moves, which mechanically favors détente end-states. Treat the F1 range of 7–30% and the F2 range of 12–45% as irreducible, and **weight your hedges toward the ~45% of combined probability mass sitting on futures where conflict continues inside new rails (F3 + F1)** rather than the 28.5% where co-governed détente dominates.

The games' most durable lesson for a CFO is not that peace is coming — it is that **the war is moving to channels you are not yet measuring**, and the side that instruments the full adverse-action surface first negotiates from evidence while the other side celebrates a falling denial rate.

# 3. WHAT SURVIVED ADVERSARIAL REVIEW (findings you can take to a board)

Three findings were confirmed by **all three** peer reviewers and hold regardless of which future wins:

**3.1 Yield displacement** *(replicated in both regulatory-shock runs; confirmed against 20 years of payer behavior).* When human-review mandates or contract terms constrain the denial event, payment-integrity recovery rotates **within quarters — not contract cycles** — into prepay pends, downcodes, billing-accuracy edits, COB/subrogation, and postpay clawbacks. **Headline denial-rate improvement will materially overstate actual leakage recovery.** A corridor or dashboard that measures only initial denials is measuring the war the payer stopped fighting.

**3.2 The transparency boomerang** *(replicated in both series, on both sides; confirmed against Cigna PXDX, nH Predict, and OIG-data precedents).* Any published adjudication artifact — overturn statistics, settlement terms, error-cap disclosures, provider-armed patient-AI data — becomes adverse evidence on litigation and legislative timescales, typically **one to three years**. In the games, payer-published overturn stats became the viral "wrong three times out of five" weapon; provider-armed patient-AI platforms inverted into chargemaster-attack industries. **Publish only what you can defend being quoted against you.**

**3.3 The toll structure** *(replicated; confirmed).* No game reached the vendor-benchmark **$59 cost-per-denial** — the floor realized was ~$84–100, because prevention, audit, attestation, governance, and arbitration are **recurring tolls that replace rather than eliminate adjudication spend**. Planning implication: budget *total adverse-adjudication cost as a share of net revenue*, and expect cost-per-denial to mechanically **rise** as prevention shrinks volume. Denial ops gets cheaper per dollar protected — never cheap.

## Findings weakened on review, restated in corrected form

- **"Mutual exhaustion" is asymmetric.** Escalation is cost-ratcheting for providers and fully-insured books, but on the ~60% of commercial lives that are self-funded, payment-integrity recovery is **fee revenue for payer services arms**. Expect payers to sustain the race longer than the games showed; the off-ramp is priced by litigation, regulation, and employer abrasion — not mutual pain.
- **The "détente attractor" is a generated hypothesis, not a tested result.** Realistic near-term convergence is on **process terms payers will actually sign** — machine-readable edit disclosure, 60–90-day change notice, audit rights, gold-card entry criteria — *not* numeric denial-rate corridors, which payer counsel will strike as denial-quota exposure under state UM law.
- **Published AI-governance charters cut both ways.** They reduce regulatory tail risk but increase payer SIU-audit and qui tam exposure. Govern internally, pre-audit coding integrity, and negotiate extrapolation and lookback limits **before** any disclosure.
- **The structural casualty is the squeezed middle, not the rural tail.** Rural restraint traces to network-adequacy math and trivial rural spend. The $300M–$1B tier — too big for subsidized tiers, too small to self-build — produced the games' only distress sale, replicated across both series, and matches the real consolidation trend. (This finding carries the study's *strongest* diagnostic weight because nothing in the scoring protected or rewarded it — it emerged.)
- **There is no synchronized "2028 window."** Renewal opportunities roll contract-by-contract across 2027–2030; first-cycle wins will be wedge provisions rather than full corridor economics; and escalation indicators may peak later, not earlier.

# 4. THE BOARD BRIEF (seven sentences for a CFO)

1. **Most likely 2030: conflict inside new rails, not peace.** Regulated equilibrium plus continued arms race carry ~45% combined probability versus ~28% for a dominant negotiated détente — and the détente estimate is the study's most disputed number (12–45% judge range) because the game design itself favored cooperative endings.
2. **Falling denial rates can be a trap.** When denials are constrained by law or contract, payer recovery rotates within quarters into pends, downcodes, billing edits, COB, and clawbacks. Fund the total-adverse-action dashboard now; do not let a falling headline denial rate stand in for recovered revenue.
3. **Denial ops will get cheaper per dollar protected, never cheap.** Prevention and governance tolls replace appeal spend rather than eliminating it; the $59 cost-per-denial vendor benchmark is not achievable, and cost-per-denial will mechanically rise as volume falls.
4. **Anything we publish will be quoted against us within one to three years** — confirmed by both game series and real precedent (Cigna PXDX, nH Predict). Adopt AI governance and coding-integrity audits internally and immediately, but gate all publication through counsel and negotiate audit lookback caps before any self-disclosure.
5. **The renewal calendar is the leverage map.** Corridor-type terms bind only at renewal, on a rolling 2027–2030 schedule. Pre-draft the package now (edit-library disclosure, 60–90-day change notice, audit rights, gold-card criteria); expect wedge provisions in cycle one and full economics in cycle two. Numeric denial-rate corridors are not payer-signable — do not anchor on them.
6. **If we are, own, or partner with a $300M–$1B system:** that tier — not rural hospitals — was the structural casualty in both game series. Secure consortium affiliation or corridor-entry terms before renewal leverage erodes; for rural affiliates, the survival variable is a provider-side alternative existing *before* a payer delegation offer reaches the board.
7. **Assume the arms race pays the other side.** On self-funded business, payment-integrity recovery is fee revenue for payer services arms, so do not expect payer exhaustion to end the conflict; the off-ramps are litigation pricing, state AI-UR statutes, CMS-0057-F enforcement, and employer abrasion — invest in evidence for all four channels.

# 5. STRATEGY: WHAT TO DO

## 5.1 No-regret moves (tiered after peer review)

**TIER 1 — genuinely no-regret (pays in ≥4 of 5 futures; start now):**
1. **Build the total-adverse-action measurement layer.** Pend aging, downcode dollar impact, recoupment/clawback volume, underpayments, and timely-filing losses on the same dashboard as initial denials — by payer, monthly. The single most replicated blind spot in both series was providers measuring the war the payer had stopped fighting. This is the prerequisite for every other move.
2. **Pre-draft the renewal package, mapped to your actual renewal calendar through 2029.** Machine-readable edit-library appendices with 60–90-day change notice, audit rights, model-change disclosure, gold-carding entry criteria, and total-adverse-action measurement perimeters. Drafting is cheap; latecomers ratify terms others authored.
3. **Implement CMS-0057-F above minimum, scoped to what payer endpoints will realistically serve** — and capture the data: payer-by-payer API scorecards, denial-reason archives, overturn telemetry. Mandated spend either way; above-minimum converts it into negotiating evidence.
4. **Route every payer delegation or subsidized-services offer through counsel** for data-rights, exclusivity, and network-steering review before any affiliate board votes. In the games, this single screen was repeatedly the difference between independence and capture.

**TIER 2 — conditional (do them, with the stated gates):**
5. **Internal AI governance now; publication gated by counsel.** Autonomy tiers, human decision floors, error caps with sampling audits, self-report-and-rollback protocol — plus a preemptive third-party coding-integrity audit with quiet repayment of drift (the "upcoding inoculation" that defused the acuity-inflation narrative in every game it was pre-positioned). Publish selectively, after negotiating audit lookback limits.
6. **Shared-services utility for small/rural affiliates,** chartered through a familiar vehicle (state hospital association), with explicit data-portability and non-exclusivity terms — started now, with honest 3–5-year expectations. In all eight games, rural survival was conditional on this BATNA existing when the payer compact arrived.

**TIER 3 — strategic bets, priced as such:**
7. A pooled, antitrust-screened denial observatory with peer systems; redeploy-first staffing for denial-ops (retraining toward prevention analytics, documentation, and arbitration support).

# 8. LIMITATIONS (disclosed in full — the peer reviewers' own words, condensed)

1. **Pseudo-replication.** The two series shared the same evidence brief, archetypes, adjudication doctrine, and seeds (with demonstrable narrative overlap). Cross-series convergence is consistency across two runs of a shared design, not independent confirmation; effective sample size is closer to four seed conditions under one doctrine than eight games.
2. **Convergence artifact.** The doctrine hard-codes that symmetric escalation cancels and cooperative moves are the only route to large metric shifts; 7-of-8 F2 convergence is close to the unique rational outcome under those scoring rules. The inside/outside splits (F1 7↔30%, F2 45↔12%) are irreducible ranges, not averaging errors.
3. **Scoring-protected outcomes.** Prairie's solvency was a Blue victory condition (its closure double-counted toward shared loss) — "Prairie survived 8 of 8" is a designed attractor. Conversely, the squeezed-middle casualty and yield displacement were *unscored emergent results* and carry the study's strongest diagnostic weight.
4. **One White Cell world-model.** All second-order effects (courts, AGs, media, patient-AI growth) came from a single umpire sampled eight times; no run tested a passive, slow, or hostile regulatory environment. Real regulatory cycles run 3–7 years; the federal posture through 2028 leans deregulatory.
5. **Payer economics under-specified.** MLR mechanics and self-funded ASO fee structures were absent, overstating payer pain from escalation; employers — the payer's actual customer — were never independently modeled. Real payers manage PMPM trend, PI yield, Stars, and employer retention, not denial rates.
6. **Seven actors vs. thousands.** Every détente needed at most seven signatures; historical multilateral coordination base rates (CAQH CORE 15+ yrs; ~3% Texas gold-card uptake) imply the games' four-year convergence is optimistic by roughly 2–4x. Numeric denial-rate corridors are likely not payer-signable under state UM law at all.
7. **False quantitative precision & timeline compression.** End-state weights, escalation indices, $84/$59 figures, and "2x displacement" are umpire-assigned constructs; three turns forced 10–15-year institutional processes into four years. If milestones slip one contract cycle, the displacement, boomerang, and toll findings survive; the détente-attractor and front-loaded-escalation findings do not in current form.
8. **Endogenous futures taxonomy.** Games were seeded from and judged against the same five-future frame; futures outside it (federal preemption, a payer data-breach trust shock, vertical integration absorbing the provider side) were structurally invisible.
9. **Zero-event inferences** ("F1 never finished primary") are 0-of-8 observations under a doctrine that disfavored those outcomes — reported as "not observed under these conditions," never as base rates.
